Cold chain temperatures and monitoring

Vaccines and food spoil invisibly when the cold chain breaks, so the temperature limits and continuous monitoring are the standard. WHO prequalification is the bar for vaccine storage in health programmes; the EN cold-chain recorder standards are the reference for food. Holdover and freeze protection matter most off-grid and in cold climates.

Specification

Vaccine storage: +2 to +8 C in refrigerators or freezers prequalified by WHO (PQS category E003, which includes solar direct-drive models), each with temperature monitoring meeting WHO PQS E006 (for example a 30-day electronic temperature logger). An Applied Commons vaccine-fridge design does not replace a prequalified one in a health programme until it has been evaluated against E003. Food: chilled food at or below the national limit (commonly +5 C or +8 C); frozen food at -18 C or colder. Temperature recorders and thermometers for food storage and transport meet EN 12830 and EN 13485 and are verified per EN 13486. Every cold-store module logs its temperature at least every 15 minutes, alarms on any excursion, publishes the readings on ac-mqtt-iot-bus and keeps a local record that survives a power cut. Designs state their holdover time (how long the temperature holds without power) at the hottest ambient they are rated for, and their freeze protection: freezing destroys many vaccines, and the cold-climate set includes sub-zero ambients.
