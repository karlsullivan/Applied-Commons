Measuring and controlling what essential needs depend on: low-cost sensors and test equipment for water quality, air, soil, health and infrastructure, with open data and calibration; controllers and automation for pumps, irrigation, microgrids and treatment systems (open PLCs, actuators).

The need

People lacking reliably safe and controllable water, food, air, and health services, concentrated in low- and middle-income countries: 2.2B (27% of the world) lack safely managed drinking water (only 74% had it in 2024); agriculture's users withdraw ~70% of global freshwater (industry ~20%, domestic ~12%), with groundwater supplying ~25% of irrigation water; 99% of the global population breathe air exceeding WHO limits (only ~6000 cities in 117 countries monitor on the ground); and cardiovascular/respiratory disease in LMICs is detected at the point of care via low-cost ECG and pulse oximetry. Safety/infrastructure users are off-grid communities and small operators of pumps, treatment plants and microgrids.

High and persistent. Unsafe water, sanitation and hygiene cause 1.4M deaths and 74M DALYs per year — 2.5% of all global deaths and 2.9% of global DALYs (GBD 2019 / Lancet 2023). Where water quality, soil moisture, air or grid health is not measured, contamination, over/under-irrigation, pollution and faults are undetected and unmanaged, so the harm recurs and is invisible; exposure is worst in low- and middle-income countries.

What people do now

Measurement is dominated by periodic laboratory analysis: a basic bacteria+chemistry panel at a US state-certified lab is US$150-300 per sample (full panels US$150-700+, PFAS +US$300-600) with 5-10 day turnaround, and rarer/costlier where labs are scarce. Irrigation without sensing runs on fixed schedules or guesswork. Air monitoring exists only in ~6000 cities, leaving the vast majority of exposed people unmeasured. Pumps, treatment and microgrids are switched manually or via proprietary controllers. This is accurate where it exists but too slow, sparse and costly per person reached to match the scale of the unmet need.

Where existing solutions fall short

Commercial/lab instruments are accurate but expensive, slow (5-10 day lab turnaround) and too sparse to reach the unmet need. Low-cost sensors close the price gap, but systematic reviews find few are validated against professional reference devices, so real-world reliability and accuracy under drift, fouling and field calibration remain the open problem. Open controllers and grid-forming inverter software (openHAB, OpenIBR) exist but are not yet packaged, certified or trusted as the default control layer for essential infrastructure like pumps, treatment plants and microgrids. The connective gap is the calibrated, open, locally buildable middle: cheap enough to deploy at scale, validated enough to be trusted for a life-safety decision.

What a solution must do

  • Water/safety: continuous field turbidity + pH/conductivity/chlorine with ~1 NTU accuracy over 0-100 NTU at ~US$64 in parts (reference: the validated low-cost continuous turbidity monitor), calibrated against a reference instrument, so a water source is verified safe in real time rather than once a season.
  • Food: soil-moisture sensing (capacitive/Arduino class) accurate enough to drive irrigation scheduling across a whole plot, so a 50 W-class irrigation pump runs only when the crop needs water — reducing the ~70% of freshwater that agriculture withdraws.
  • Air: low-cost community PM2.5/PM10 (and NO2) sensors at neighborhood resolution, to close the gap between the ~6000 monitoring cities and the rest of the 99% exposed, so exposure is actionable where people live.
  • Health: low-cost, reliable ECG and pulse oximetry at the point of care, in the class shown cost-effective for peri-operative use in low-income settings, so dominant LMIC cardiovascular/respiratory conditions are detectable without a hospital.
  • Safety/infrastructure: open, locally maintainable controllers (open PLCs, open firmware) with open communication standards (Modbus/MQTT) and open grid-forming inverter control to drive pumps, irrigation valves, treatment plants and microgrids, with fail-safe protection that keeps them running and shuts them down safely on fault.
  • Calibration & open data: a reproducible low-equipment calibration routine and an open data format so readings from different deployments are comparable (the recurring literature failure is low-cost sensors never validated against reference devices).

Constraints

  • Power & connectivity: must run on small off-grid (solar/battery) power, tolerate no or intermittent backhaul, and store data locally to survive disconnection.
  • Calibration & drift: field sensors drift; a trusted solution needs a repeatable calibration routine and open data so deployments remain comparable.
  • Durability & local maintainability: rugged, cheap, repairable with locally available parts and tools; open schematics and firmware so a local technician can fix rather than replace.
  • Openness / anti-lock-in: open data standards, open control code, no proprietary cloud, so a community is not held by a vendor or a defunct API.
  • Unit cost: component-level cost must stay in the tens of US$ per node (not hundreds) to be deployable at the scale of 2.2B people / 70% of freshwater / 99% air exposure.
