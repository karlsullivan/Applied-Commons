The need

645M hungry (2025, 7.8% of world); ~2.1B (25.8%) moderate/severe food-insecure; 2.69B (32.7%) cannot afford a healthy diet. Africa worst: 309M hungry (20.0% of pop), 56.6% food-insecure, 66.6% cannot afford a healthy diet. Child: 149-150M under-5 stunted, 45M wasted (2022/2026), 1 in 3 under-5 malnourished. 318M in acute hunger across WFP's 68 countries; famines in parts of Gaza and Sudan. 2.1B cook on open fires/inefficient stoves (923M in sub-Saharan Africa); >750M lack electricity.

Severe and persistent, with pockets worsening. Nearly half of under-5 deaths are linked to undernutrition (mostly LMICs); a malnourished child is up to 12x more likely to die. Unsafe food: 866M illnesses and 1.52M deaths in 2021, US$310B losses, under-5s carry 29% of the burden. Household cooking pollution: 2.9M deaths/yr (309k under-5), 95M DALYs, hit hardest by women/children. SOFI 2026: progress too slow for 2030; adult obesity rose 12.1%->16.2% (2012-2024); anaemia worsening; Middle East conflict could keep 510-520M hungry into 2030.

What people do now

Cooking: 2.1B use polluting fuels/inefficient stoves, costing significant time for cooking and fuel gathering, displacing education/income and concentrating injury and respiratory harm on women and children. Post-harvest: ~30% of global food production lost/wasted yearly, driven by smallholder lack of technology and market access. Food safety: WHO Five Keys (keep clean, separate raw/cooked, cook thoroughly, safe temperatures, safe water) is the 88-language baseline but adherence is uneven (866M illnesses still). Diet: most low-income diets miss the WHO adequate-diet benchmark on cost alone, with animal-source foods and fruit/veg being the most expensive and most often absent; only 30.8% of 6-23-month-olds get a minimally diverse diet.

Where existing solutions fall short

Affordability: healthy diet unaffordable for ~2.7B (32.7%) and its cost rose 2.94 (2017) -> 4.28 (2025) PPP $/day; worst in Africa (66.6%) - price cuts alone are insufficient but it is the dominant constraint. Adequacy in the critical window: only 30.8% of 6-23-month-olds get a minimally diverse diet and stunting/wasting/anaemia/obesity targets are all missed. Clean preparation: 2.1B still cook on polluting fuels, 1.8B will by 2030 - cheap clean locally-buildable cooking/preservation energy is not reaching rural low-income households. Post-harvest: ~30% loss driven by smallholder lack of technology and market access - existing cold-chain/storage is too costly or power/logistics-dependent. Production inputs: rising fertilizer cost (+35%) and weather squeeze the poorest households. (FAO food-loss/waste and SOFI detail pages could not be retrieved; the 30% loss figure is from the WFP page.)

What a solution must do

  • Enable a diet meeting WHO healthy-diet thresholds using local foods: >=400 g/day fruit+veg (adults), free sugars <10% of energy, salt <5 g/day, fat 15-30% of energy, fibre >=25 g/day
  • Make that diet affordable for low-income households, materially below the 4.28 PPP $/day healthy-diet cost and far below the 66.6%-of-Africa unaffordable level
  • Reduce post-harvest loss on the foods handled (vs the ~30% currently lost), with a measurable shelf-life extension on a defined staple
  • Reduce the dominant foodborne pathogens (E. coli/ETEC, Shigella, Campylobacter, Listeria) on the target food toward the WHO Five Keys outcome, using safe water and safe temperatures
  • Be clean at the point of use for cooking/energy - meet WHO indoor-air emission targets (solar, electricity, biogas, LPG, natural gas, alcohol, or a compliant biomass stove) - and operable by a non-specialist with basic tools/skills
  • Be locally buildable: materials and assembly available and repairable locally, operable without reliable grid power or reliable internet

Constraints

  • Power: >750M lack electricity; 49% of rural vs 14% of urban households rely on polluting fuels, so any grid-dependent solution excludes the hardest segment
  • Connectivity/literacy: rural and low-connectivity; must use short, validated, multilingual, low-literacy instructions (Five Keys model), not app dependence
  • Cost: design for a household that cannot pay 4.28 PPP $/day for a healthy diet; material cost low and self-financing via reduced loss or fuel/time savings
  • Skills: operate with basic local skills (metalwork, masonry, drying/curing, simple fermentation)
  • Climate/inputs: robust to heat (rising foodborne risk); El Nino could cut rice 20-50% (mid-2026); fertilizer +35% YoY H1 2026; 45% of low-income countries had >5% food inflation - avoid scarce/price-volatile inputs
  • Scalability: 1.8B will still lack clean cooking by 2030 at current rates, so the solution must be cheap and scalable, not a premium niche product
