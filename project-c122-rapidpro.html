Open-source mobile-messaging platform (originally UNICEF RapidSMS 2007, open-sourced with TextIt 2014; Digital Public Good 2020) for designing and scaling interactive apps over SMS, WhatsApp and voice/IVR on basic phones. Powers governments and development partners: 130 UNICEF country offices, 284 active workspaces, 5+ billion messages; used for early warning, health programs and market/advisory flows. Very mature, documented, localizable to many languages; SMS/IVR makes it work on feature phones and offline-friendly. Strong fit for the brief's SMS/IVR and localization requirements.

FailureEffectMitigation
Burst throttling during a mass crisis sendThe broadcast exceeds the channel's MPS; the provider returns ESME_RTHROTTLED (SMPP error 0x00000058) or queues, so late in the event some warnings never arrive on time.Pre-provision MPS headroom (short code ~100x a toll-free line on AWS; long code gated by approved 10DLC campaign), size Courier workers to the target MPS, batch the send, and use exponential backoff on throttle instead of immediate retry.
Multi-part (segmented) SMS for long warningsMessages longer than ~3x140 bytes split into parts, cost more, deliver slower, and can arrive incomplete or out of order.Keep life-safety alerts short and single-part; test the exact byte length; use MMS only where acceptable (cheaper than 3+ SMS parts per carrier docs).
Carrier / registry gating (10DLC in the US, DLT in India)Unregistered Sender ID, unapproved campaign, or unapproved template gets traffic rejected, so the warning is blocked regardless of correct RapidPro configuration.Register the campaign, templates, and sender ID with the carrier/operator before go-live; keep it approved and in good standing.
Recipients with no cellular signalSMS cannot reach a phone with no signal at all (it is not a no-cell-signal channel), so the '100% at-risk' target silently fails for that subset.Pair RapidPro SMS with a non-cellular channel (cell broadcast, radio, siren) for the no-signal population; store-and-forward only helps phones that are temporarily in range.
One-way broadcast with no confirmation loopIf replies are not collected the operator cannot confirm who was reached; the warning degrades into a broadcast (a documented failure of the 2024 Accra floods).Use two-way flows with SAFE/HELP keywords, automatic no-response follow-up, and a live coordination dashboard; treat low confirmation rates as the trigger for ground follow-up.
Operator cost/throughput surprise at scalePer-SMS fees vary widely by country/network (roughly ~$0.006 in Nigeria up to ~$0.40 in Somalia; Ghana ~$0.007 at business tier) and operator hosting is separate ($7,000-9,000/yr via a managed vendor, SMS billed per country), so an under-budgeted campaign can stall mid-event.Budget per-SMS + hosting + labor separately; estimate the campaign against a live per-country rate index before committing.

Known gaps

What the original design does not document:

  • No hardware or cloud-instance prices retrieved - the hosting doc gives CPU/RAM specs but no supplier or region pricing, so all compute unit_costs are null.
  • RapidPro does not ship a single docker-compose / machine-readable BOM file at the repo root (docker/ and compose files 404 on main); the deployment is documented in prose in the hosting page, not as a BOM file.
  • rp-archiver and rp-indexer are referenced only by name in the hosting doc - no dedicated repo/README was retrieved to confirm their build steps or version pins.
  • Voice/IVR per-minute carrier rates not retrieved (the AWS page covers throughput limits, not price).
  • The reference Ghana deployment (rapidpro.app) is a hypothetical/marketing scenario, not a verified operational install - it illustrates scale, not an audited deployment.
  • No production-grade BOM or hardware sizing: the only reference compose (nyaruka/rapidpro-docker) is explicitly 'for development and documentation purposes only, not intended for production', and upstream gives no CPU/RAM/disk sizing for Postgres 15, Elasticsearch 9.3.3, Valkey 8.1, or the app/celery/mailroom/courier containers at target scale.
  • No self-hosted hosting cost model: upstream publishes no price for self-hosting; the only published prices are a third-party managed-hosting offer (rapidpro.app: USD 7,000/yr NGO, USD 9,000/yr Corporate, setup USD 1,500-3,000) which is not the upstream project's own.
  • No SMS carrier per-message rate card from upstream: SMS cost is per-message and carrier/country dependent (third-party Africa A2P index shows roughly USD 0.006-0.04 per SMS by country and volume tier), but RapidPro itself does not document message-unit pricing.
  • No no-cell-signal delivery channel: the channel-type tree has SMS/IVR/WhatsApp/etc. but no cell-broadcast, radio, or siren channel type, so the design cannot document reaching people with zero cellular coverage (a core requirement in the brief).
  • Localization data is not consistent across the two lines: the frozen canonical rapidpro/rapidpro ships 7 UI locales (cs, en_US, es, fr, mn, pt_BR, ru) while the active nyaruka/temba fork ships only 4 (en_US, es, fr, pt_BR) and compiles .po but not .mo; the brief's '7 languages' claim only matches the frozen line, so a deployment of the active fork does not deliver cs/mn/ru out of the box.
  • Mailroom and Courier (and the active temba fork) are now Business Source License 1.1 (Change Date 2028-11-09 to AGPL-3.0): upstream does not document an OSS build path for the current components, so a purely open-source deployment would have to use the older AGPL-3.0 rapidpro/mailroom line instead of the actively developed nyaruka components.
  • No documented data-retention/archival sizing: rp-archiver and the Localstack S3/DynamoDB emulators in the reference compose have no documented object/bucket sizing or long-term storage cost for message and archive history.
  • No documented end-to-end acceptance checklist that maps to life-safety requirements; the docs cover architecture and hosting, not how to verify a finished instance against a warning use case.
  • No no-cell-broadcast/radio/siren channel type exists in the codebase (the 66-type channel tree is entirely cellular/app/voice), so the brief's '100% at-risk incl. no-cell-signal' requirement is structurally unmet and is not documented as a known limitation.
  • Only 7 UI locales ship (cs, en_US, es, fr, mn, pt_BR, ru); the cost/effort of authoring an arbitrary local-language warning is undocumented, and content is authored per locale rather than auto-localized.
  • No load/throughput pass-fail benchmark or load-test harness is provided upstream; the hosting page gives worker counts for 'millions of messages per week' but no measurable load target.
  • No documented periodic safety/drill procedure (e.g. a regular all-hands SMS drill) to keep the crisis path warm.
  • Operator-side cost model (hosting + per-SMS + labor) is only available from third-party vendors (e.g. rapidpro.app pricing); upstream explicitly discourages self-hosting but gives no cost figure.

Safety

The build pack is a software deployment guide and covers none of the physical safety. Self-hosting a multi-server cluster on mains power in a home, farm or village introduces real physical hazards, the dominant one electrical: the mains connection is a licensed-electrician step (the task's own 'mains wiring' example), and the pack lists no UPS/backup power and no facility controls. If the self-hosted path is taken, the verdict is qualified-person (licensed electrician for the electrical work, plus the listed controls). If rapidpro.app managed hosting is used instead, the vendor installs and operates the hardware, so these physical hazards are offloaded and the operator's residual exposure is effectively ok. The software itself (SMS/IVR over a feature phone) carries no physical hazard to the user.

HazardSeverityControls
The self-hosted cluster must be wired to mains power. In a home, farm or village a high-load rack connected without proper circuit sizing, earthing (PE) and overcurrent/RCD protection creates a shock, thermal and overcurrent hazard. A running server cluster generates continuous heat; in a small, unventilated room in a village this can overheat equipment and ignite combustibles (electrical fire origin).mediumDedicated ventilated/air-conditioned space with temperature monitoring; No combustibles or storage in the server space; CO2 or dry-chemical extinguisher (never water on live equipment) and a fire alarm
A loaded 19-inch rack is heavy (hundreds of kg); during unloading and installation in a village without equipment it poses a crushing/lifting hazard and, if not anchored, a tip-over hazard.mediumAnchor/bolt the rack to the floor before loading; Lift with multiple people or a dolly; load heaviest equipment at the bottom; Check the floor's load capacity before placing the rack
The BOM lists no UPS, but village mains is often unstable so backup power is likely to be added. UPS batteries (lead-acid VRLA or lithium iron phosphate) can vent, overheat, thermal-runaway (Li) or leak if deep-discharged, overcharged or poorly maintained.mediumCharge in a ventilated space with temperature monitoring; Never deep-discharge or overcharge; follow the manufacturer charge profile; Treat the battery as hazardous waste at end of life
If a lead-acid UPS battery is added, its sulfuric acid electrolyte causes severe skin/eye burns and corrosion, and the battery is hazardous waste.mediumGloves and eye protection when handling; acid spill kit and eyewash nearby; Dispose as hazardous waste, not in the general trash
If a diesel/gasoline generator is added for backup power (the diesel-UPS 'DRUPS' pattern), running it indoors or near an opening produces carbon monoxide, which is lethal.highGenerator outdoors only, exhaust directed away from windows and openings; CO detector near the server space; never run in a closed room or garage

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Architecture that bounds throughput: the message hot path is the Go service 'mailroom' (not Django/Celery). RapidPro enqueues work into Redis — per-contact queues c:<org>:<contact> for inbound message handling, plus 'batch' and 'handler' queues for broadcasts/flow starts — and mailroom consumes them via a Foreman/Worker pool. Defaults are 4 batch workers and 32 handler workers (goroutines), configurable via env; scaling is done by adding mailroom instances and raising worker counts. Celery is used only for shared tasks (media upload, exports, contact import, channel interrupt) and cron maintenance.
  • RapidPro has 68 channel types (temba/channels/types) as of 2026-11-09 and every one is point-to-point (Twilio, WhatsApp, SMS carriers such as Africa's Talking/Bandwidth/Infobip, Kannel, Firebase, and voice channels); there is no broadcast, CMAS, cell-broadcast, radio, satellite, or mesh channel type, so there is no native path to a phone with no cellular coverage.
  • The earlier claim of a 'direct RapidPro-to-CMAS cell-broadcast bridge' (that the android-channel README 'explicitly names the UNHCR's cell broadcast integration') is a FABRICATION and is dropped: a full read of the android-channel README shows only a GSM-modem SMS fallback with no mention of UNHCR, CMAS, cell broadcast, or any broadcast integration, and greps of the android-channel and rapidpro codebases for those terms return zero matches. No such bridge exists in the code, docs, or public issues.
  • Aggregate national-scale volume is documented: 5+ billion messages exchanged, 130 UNICEF country offices, 284 active RapidPro workspaces (figures from RapidPro's 10-year/2024 impact page). These are cumulative, aggregate counts — they carry no information about send rate, per-event latency, or how the system behaved under load.
  • Applying 2% of monthly GNI per capita to 14 low-income countries (World Bank GNI per capita, Atlas method, 2024) yields a per-person monthly affordability ceiling ranging from ~US$0.92 (Mozambique, GNI pc $550) to ~US$4.20 (Cote d'Ivoire, GNI pc $2,520). Annualised this is ~US$11 to ~US$50 per person.
  • Absence across public GitHub: 0 issues/PRs for the exact phrases 'CMAS', 'cell broadcast', 'satellite', and 'mesh network' in both the rapidpro and nyaruka orgs (stable across 3 runs). The only nonzero signal, 4 unquoted 'cell broadcast' matches in nyaruka, was inspected and is the word 'broadcast' used in RapidPro's internal 'Broadcasts' message-batch UI pages (temba-components/temba PRs), not cellular-broadcast technology.
  • The 'External API' channel (code EX, 'Connect to an external service to send and receive messages') posts per contact (to/text/from) and is the generic HTTP integration surface an external CMAS/cell-broadcast, LoRa, or mesh gateway would hook into. It is the honest closest native option, but it is a workaround: the zero-coverage last mile is done by the third-party system, not by RapidPro.
  • The Android Channel (AGPL, Nyaruka/UNICEF 2014-2023) is a phone-as-modem GSM SMS relay that uses the device's own existing cell plan ('using the cell phone plan you already have'); it still requires cellular on that phone and therefore does not extend reach into no-coverage areas.
