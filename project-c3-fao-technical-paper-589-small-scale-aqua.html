FAO Fisheries & Aquaculture Technical Paper No. 589 (Somerville et al., 2014, 288 pp, ISBN 978-92-5-108532-5), open access. Authoritative small-scale design/sizing/parts reference: nitrogen cycle and unit balancing, water-quality parameters and sourcing, unit design across the three methods (media beds, NFT, deep water culture), fish/plant/bacteria management and troubleshooting, local/sustainable inputs, and nine appendices including tools to size biofilter media and ammonia load for a given stocking density/feed, homemade fish-feed production, and a cost-benefit analysis of a small-scale media-bed unit. Best used as the sizing + costing backbone. (Retrieved abstract does not explicitly address cold-climate operation.)

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • TP 589 contains zero quantitative microbiological data. Whole-document term sweep of the official PDF: CFU=0, MPN=0, colony count=0, plate count=0, membrane filtration=0, log reduction=0, '99.9'=0, 'Escherichia'=2, 'Salmonella'=2, 'E. coli'=0, bacteriolog*=0. There is therefore no measured E. coli or Salmonella reduction (or any pathogen measurement) for produce or fish under the paper's hygiene protocol.
  • TP 589's only E. coli/Salmonella mentions are two qualitative risk sentences in §5.3.3 'Pathogenic bacteria' (printed p.79 / PDF p.101): warm-blooded animals are the major E. coli risk source, birds often carry Salmonella spp., and dangerous bacteria can enter with animal faeces. This is a hazard statement, not a measurement.
  • TP 589's 'GAPs hygiene protocol' (§8.6.2 'Food safety', printed p.137 / PDF p.159) is a behavioural checklist only: always be clean, hand-washing, sanitise harvest equipment, do not let water touch produce, wash produce after harvest and before consumption, keep soil/faeces/vermin/birds/pets out, exclusion netting, treat rainwater if birds roost, disposable gloves. It specifies no target concentration, no monitoring parameter for E. coli/Salmonella, and reports no measurement of any kind.
  • FAO TP 589 cost model: fish feed is the largest opex item at USD 11.25/mo (4.5 kg x USD 2.50/kg) of USD 26.45/mo total operating cost = 42.5% of opex. Capital is USD 700, yearly net profit USD 441, payback 19 months. The paper explicitly says 'supplementing fish feed' 'will considerably decrease the payback period.' (FAO Appendix 7, Tables A7.1/A7.2, full text)
  • The '32% protein requirement' is the FAO cost-model assumption ('good-quality 32 percent protein feed', Appendix 7). FAO Sec 7.2.1 states omnivorous grow-out fish (tilapia, common carp) need 25-35% protein, and Appendix 5 Table A5.1 lists optimal protein of 30% for Nile tilapia and 32% for common carp. So a 30-32% CP locally-made diet is within spec for the tilapia model.
  • The standard FAO 589 small media-bed unit (1,000 L fish tank + 3 m2 growing space) runs on ~35 W average: a submersible water pump specified at 2,000 L/h at 1.5 m head drawing 25-50 W (rule of thumb ~40 L/h per W of pump power) plus a 5 W air pump; FAO's cost-benefit analysis prices electricity at 25 kWh/month (30 W water pump + 5 W air pump x 24 h x 30 days). This puts the whole unit comfortably inside the 25-100 W band the question asks about, and the circulation pump is the dominant load, so an aerator-only design is strictly cheaper to power.
  • FAO TP 589 (2014) capital cost for the reference household media-bed unit (1000 L IBC tank + 3 m2 growing space): USD 700 one-time (Table A7.1, printed p. 206). All six line items (IBC tanks 200, electrical equipment 120, media-bed support 80, volcanic gravel 120, miscellaneous 100, plumbing 80) independently sum to 700.
  • Continuous operating cost is USD 26.45/month = USD 317.40/year (Table A7.2, p. 206): purchased fish feed USD 135/year (42.5%, single largest cost), fingerlings USD 60/year, seedlings USD 42/year, electricity USD 30/year (35 W continuous, 25 kWh/month at 0.10 USD/kWh), water USD 14.40/year, miscellaneous USD 36/year. Every item recomputed from the table's own unit quantities and unit prices and matching.
