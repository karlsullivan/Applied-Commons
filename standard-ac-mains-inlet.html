Mains power for modules: external supply first, IEC 60320 inlet otherwise

Mains power for modules: external supply first, IEC 60320 inlet otherwise
Codeac-mains-inlet
Kindelectrical
StatusApproved
ApprovedOctober 2026

Mains is the most dangerous interface a module can have, and none of the standards covered it. Using a certified external supply keeps mains out of most modules altogether; where it must come in, an IEC 60320 inlet means one module design works in every country with a locally bought cord. The RCD and qualified-person rules are the established controls for shock and fire.

Specification

Modules that run from the grid take mains power in this order of preference. (1) A certified external power supply (IEC 62368-1, or IEC 61558 for transformers) feeding one of the extra-low-voltage standards (ac-dc-usb-c, ac-poe-power-data, ac-dc-bus-elv), so no mains enters the module; this is the default. (2) Where mains must enter the module, an IEC 60320 appliance inlet, so the country-specific plug stays on a standard detachable cord: C14 (10 A, with protective earth) for Class I equipment; C8 (2.5 A, no earth) only for double-insulated Class II equipment; C20 (16 A) for loads above 10 A; a hot-condition coupler where the inlet runs above 70 C. The inlet carries a fuse sized to the module and, where practical, a switch. Supply voltages per IEC 60038: 230 V 50 Hz (Europe, Africa, most of Asia) or 120 V 60 Hz (most of the Americas); each module states what it accepts, and wide-range 100-240 V inputs are preferred. Protection classes per IEC 61140: exposed metal of Class I equipment is bonded to the inlet's earth. Mains circuits inside a module are segregated from extra-low-voltage circuits and covered so live parts cannot be touched (see ac-din-rail-mount). Installation: socket circuits that feed modules have 30 mA RCD protection (additional protection under IEC 60364-4-41) and are wired by a qualified person under the local wiring rules; outdoor sockets and cords are rated for the location (ac-ip44-enclosure). A build pack that puts mains inside a module marks that step for a qualified person and includes an earth-continuity and insulation-resistance test.
