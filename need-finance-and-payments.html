Ways to pay for, finance and sustain what essential needs depend on: mobile money, savings groups, pay-as-you-go models, cooperative accounting tools.

The need

1.4 billion adults remain unbanked (three out of four adults have an account); 65% of adults in low-income economies lack even a basic transaction account (World Bank). 165 million unbanked adults receive private-sector wages in cash only (Global Findex 2021). Health: OOP payments are >25% of current health spending in 31 of the WHO African Region's countries (2019), >50% in 11, >70% in 3; 926.6M people globally incurred catastrophic OOP health spending (>10% of household budget) in 2015. Payments rails: 2 billion registered mobile-money accounts with >500M monthly active users (2024, GSMA); 733M people remain off-grid, mainly rural and low-income (GOGLA); 30M members in VSLAs across 67 countries; ~3M cooperatives with >1B members and 280M jobs (ICA).

Severe and worsening: catastrophic health spending rose 3.6%/yr (10% threshold) and 5.3%/yr (25% threshold) 2000-2015 and will keep rising to 2030 at current pace; OOP spending pushed 89.7M people into extreme poverty (<$1.90/day), 98.8M below $3.20/day and 183.2M into relative poverty (2015). Water: 36% of rural water points in 20 African countries are nonfunctional; in a Ghana pilot, households ran $33 average arrears (a third of annual water) and stations collected 66 cents per dollar; in Gambia, when a village solar water scheme broke, people used worm-infested wells or bought bagged water at 400x the prepaid price. Remittances - a core coping channel - still cost ~6% on average.

What people do now

Cash and informal savings dominate where no account exists (65% of low-income-economy adults have none). Remittances move via cash and agents at ~6% cost ($630B to EMDEs in 2022). VSLAs: self-managed 10-25 person groups keep paper records; CARE's program saved $1.8B and lent $105M in 2025. Mobile money (e.g. M-PESA) is the dominant digital rail in sub-Saharan Africa. Energy: PAYGo households pay small installments instead of an upfront solar purchase; lenders finance receivables. Food: smallholders buy inputs from informal credit (traders, moneylenders, family) because formal rural finance is limited; credit that works doubles crop yields via fertilizer adoption (Uganda), and mobile savings plus VSLAs raise investment (FAO brief, 43 experimental studies). Water utilities rely on cash collection agents and monthly bills, with arrears and 36% of rural points nonfunctional.

Where existing solutions fall short

65% of low-income-economy adults have no account at all, while products are priced and UX'd for the 74% who do; 165M unbanked adults get cash-only wages and no on-ramp. VSLAs and cooperatives run on paper: ICA states there is no global-level comprehensive database of cooperative statistics, and no open, local-language, group-accounting digital public good exists. PAYGo lenders still lack debt financing and rely on equity, and credit/repayment stress is already raising costs for the customers least able to pay (Acumen, 2026). Water utilities get bespoke commercial prepaid systems (e.g. US$1,000 POS terminals) instead of an open metering+payment+accounting stack; eWATERpay proves the offline ~US$0.01/20L model but targets 7M people by 2030 versus a global need, and 36% of rural water points are nonfunctional partly for collection reasons. Open core banking exists (Apache Fineract/Mifos X: cloud-ready, open APIs, on-premise) but deployment/configuration/local-language UX burden remains high and savings-group/cooperative accounting (patronage, member statements, loan cycles) is not first-class. In health, OOP remains the dominant financing mode in most African countries and household-level pooled-prepayment rails are fragmented across mobile-money providers with no common low-cost standard.

What a solution must do

  • Health: emergency liquidity a household can access same-day, with per-event fees at or below 1% of the amount, so a health shock does not push OOP above 10% of household budget (SDG 3.8.2 threshold)
  • Health: prepaid/credit-usable payment rails for clinics and pharmacies (mobile money plus offline USSD/SMS fallback) for medicines - the leading driver of catastrophic OOP spending
  • Water: prepaid metering that works offline, priced at the eWATERpay benchmark of ~US$0.01 per 20 litres for the poorest, with a ~8% local-retailer commission and rules that a missed payment does not cut supply
  • Water: digital collection that lifts recovery from the 66-cents-per-dollar pre-pilot baseline to at least 95 cents and clears arrears backlogs of the ~US$33 per-household order seen in Ghana
  • Food: to-harvest short-term credit plus mobile savings for smallholders and agri-SMEs via open core banking (Apache Fineract/Mifos X class) with group lending and mobile-money repayment, at total cost below the value of inputs it funds (evidence: credit -> fertilizer use doubled yields in Uganda)
  • Energy: PAYGo-style micro-installment financing for solar home systems serving the 733M off-grid, with repayment via mobile money and receivables securable so debt - not only equity - can underwrite it
  • Coping/safety: remittance and wage-transfer channels with end-to-end cost well under the ~6% global average (target <2%) and no unexpected fees on receipt
  • Shelter/livelihood: cooperative accounting that is open source, double-entry, re-runnable (idempotent) and readable by members in the local language, runnable on one device without cloud dependency, for the 3M-cooperative sector and 10-25 person groups
  • Savings groups: an offline-first, SMS/voice-capable digital VSLA ledger keeping savings, loans and insurance auditable by the group itself, matching the paper practice 30M members already use

Constraints

  • Offline-first: the 733M off-grid and most of the 1.4B unbanked are in low-connectivity rural areas; payments, metering and books must function with no data connection (eWATERpay runs fully offline on LoRa; Fineract supports on-premise)
  • Micro-denominations: price points of ~US$0.01 per 20L of water and weekly instalments must be payable without fee erosion or rounding loss
  • Local build and maintain: open licenses (no vendor lock), hardware local technicians can repair; commercial prepaid systems cost ~US$1,000 per terminal (Kampala) which rural utilities cannot afford
  • Behavioral: conversion to digital payments is not automatic - in Ghana mobile payments fell from ~50% of revenue within two weeks until a facilitation campaign; step-by-step facilitation is part of the solution
  • Re-runnable accounting: double-entry ledgers with idempotent, re-runnable posting so corrections never double-count, or group trust and auditability break
  • Regulatory and trust: mobile-money/savings products face country-specific licensing, and DFS pose consumer and cyber risks; personal financial data need purpose-limited access, and one in five developing-economy adults receiving wage payments into an account paid unexpected fees - fee transparency is a trust requirement
