One cable carries both power and data, so field modules need no separate mains or battery wiring. Detection makes it safe to plug anything into a PoE port, which passive PoE does not. PoE switches, injectors, splitters and Cat 5e are sold in every market. The fixes are the per-type figures the verification asked for plus the two common low-cost failure modes: passive PoE and copper-clad aluminium cable.

Specification

Power and data over one Cat 5e or better cable per IEEE 802.3-2022 Clause 33 (formerly 802.3af and 802.3at). Type 1: PSE output 44-57 V DC, 15.4 W at the PSE, 12.95 W available at the powered device (PD). Type 2 (PoE+): PSE output 50-57 V DC, 30 W at the PSE, 25.5 W at the PD. A PD accepts 37-57 V (Type 1) or 42.5-57 V (Type 2) at its input and sizes its load to the PD figure of the class it requests. Detection and classification (25 kOhm signature, power class) are mandatory, so a PSE powers only a device that presents the signature and non-PoE equipment on the network is safe. Passive PoE (a fixed 12-48 V injected without detection, common on low-cost radios) is not allowed on Applied Commons modules or ports; any such radio sits on its own labelled injector, never on an AC network port. Cable: solid-copper Cat 5e or better (IEC 61156-5); copper-clad aluminium is not allowed because it overheats under PoE. RJ45 8P8C connectors to IEC 60603-7. Outdoor runs use UV-rated cable, with surge protection where the cable enters a building. PSE: a PoE switch or midspan injector; off-grid sites use one fed from the DC bus (ac-dc-bus-elv). Safety basis: 57 V DC maximum is ES1 under IEC 62368-1:2023 and extra-low voltage under IEC 60364-4-41; equipment is designed to IEC 62368-1. Type 3/4 (up to 90 W, Clause 145) is out of scope until a module needs it.
