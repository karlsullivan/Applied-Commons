The need

Roughly 1 billion people in informal settlements (Boanada-Fuchs 2024: ~1B slum residents in 2015, +~380M by 2030), 2.1 billion people without safely managed drinking water (WHO/UNICEF JMP 2025), and the ~167 million people affected by natural disasters in 2024 alone (CRED EM-DAT 2024). The location gap is concentrated in Asia (67% of ~170,000 unmapped villages) and Africa (30%), and in fragile and least-developed contexts where safely managed water coverage is 38 percentage points lower than elsewhere (JMP 2025) and MHEWS coverage is under 50% (WMO 2023). Informal settlements also sit largely outside city administrative borders (half in Africa and the Middle East), which is exactly where the ~951 million people already covered by Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team data (across 64 countries) live alongside the remaining unmapped areas.

High and persistent. Direct disaster impact in 2024: 393 natural-hazard disasters, 16,753 deaths, 167.2 million people affected, US$241.95 billion economic damage (CRED EM-DAT 2024). Structural: countries with limited-to-moderate multi-hazard early-warning coverage have ~6x higher disaster-related mortality (4.05 vs 0.71 per 100,000) and ~5x more affected people per capita (3,132 vs 688) than countries with substantial-to-comprehensive coverage (WMO/UNDRR 2023). Only 101 countries report a multi-hazard early-warning system as of March 2023 (doubled from 2015 but still ~half the world), coverage is under 50% in LDCs/LLDCs/SIDS, and fewer than a third of countries have local-government plans to act on warnings (one-fifth in Africa, under a quarter in the Americas/Caribbean). The unmapped-village count (~170,000) is a 2024 measurement and has not been closed; it is the standing denominator for every service-delivery campaign that fails to reach a settlement that is not on a map.

What people do now

Three overlapping stop-gaps. (1) One free 24/7 UN emergency mapping service (UNOSAT-EMS, established 2003, Norway-funded, free only for the emergency phase of a declared response) that delivers satellite-derived GIS layers after a disaster has already occurred - it is reactive, donor-dependent, and cannot scale beyond the emergencies it is activated for (UNITAR midterm evaluation 2023). (2) A volunteer open-mapping movement (Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team, ~700,000 contributors, ~951 million people covered across 64 countries, ~25% of its work is disaster response) that fills the gaps the state does not fill, but depends on global volunteer bandwidth and grant funding and cannot map at the resolution or speed a national service-delivery campaign needs (100x Impact 2025/26). (3) Ad-hoc national analyses that UNICEF had to build from scratch (WASH Insecurity Analysis, 8-country ASWA programme: Burkina Faso, Cote d'Ivoire, Mali, Niger, Mozambique, Nigeria, Somalia, Tanzania, Netherlands-funded) because there is no standing, location-referenced WASH dataset at the resolution needed - a country partner's own words: 'Such details are missing' (UNICEF 2026). In the meantime, concrete service failures accumulate: in India, vaccination campaigns miss entire settlements that are not on any official map (HOT problem statement, 2025/26). The cost is borne by the affected people (deaths, displaced households, water-collection time of 30+ minutes/day for women and girls in sub-Saharan Africa and Central/Southern Asia, per JMP 2025) and by the donor budgets that keep the three stop-gaps alive.

Where existing solutions fall short

Five concrete gaps, each sourced. (1) The unmapped-village gap: ~170,000 villages have no street mapping in OpenStreetMap as of Dec 2024, 67% in Asia, 30% in Africa (Neis 2024) - this is the standing denominator for every service-delivery failure. (2) The early-warning risk-knowledge gap: only 101 countries have MHEWS (Mar 2023), coverage is under 50% in LDCs/LLDCs/SIDS, and fewer than a third of countries have local plans to act on warnings (one-fifth in Africa, under a quarter in Americas/Caribbean) (WMO/UNDRR 2023) - the 'risk knowledge' pillar is the lowest-scoring of the four. (3) The emergency-mapping capacity gap: UNOSAT-EMS is the only global 24/7 free emergency mapping service, is donor-funded by Norway, is free only in the emergency phase, and is activated reactively after a disaster has already occurred (UNITAR 2023 MTE) - there is no standing open capacity for the ~393 disasters/year (EM-DAT 2024). (4) The WASH location-data gap: UNICEF had to build the WASH Insecurity Analysis from scratch across 8 countries (Burkina Faso, Cote d'Ivoire, Mali, Niger, Mozambique, Nigeria, Somalia, Tanzania) because there is no standing, location-referenced WASH dataset at the resolution needed; a country partner's own words: 'Such details are missing' (UNICEF 2026) - against a backdrop of 2.1 billion people without safely managed drinking water (JMP 2025). (5) The informal-settlement boundary gap: only half of all slums lie inside city administrative borders (less than half in Africa and the Middle East, ~two-thirds in South Asia), and the 2015 slum population already occupied an area ~2x the size of Portugal, with ~380M more residents by 2030 (Boanada-Fuchs 2024) - administrative-boundary-based maps and planning systems structurally miss the majority of the world's informal population, and the ~951M people HOT has mapped (100x Impact 2025/26) still leave 'billions effectively invisible' per HOT's own problem statement.

What a solution must do

  • Open, village-and-building-level basemaps for the ~170,000 currently unmapped villages (67% Asia, 30% Africa) so that service-delivery campaigns (vaccination, water points, shelter) can reach every settlement - this is the safety/health/shelter requirement.
  • Open hazard-exposure and vulnerability layers at national resolution that plug directly into the four-pillar multi-hazard early-warning framework (risk knowledge / observation / dissemination / preparedness) for the ~101 countries building MHEWS and the ~half that have none, so the 'risk knowledge' pillar stops being the lowest-scoring one (WMO 2023) - this is the safety requirement.
  • Location-referenced water, sanitation and hygiene point data at community resolution for the 2.1 billion people without safely managed drinking water, so siting and upgrade decisions can be made where people actually live rather than at administrative boundaries - this is the water/sanitation requirement (JMP 2025; UNICEF WIA).
  • Real-time post-disaster damage and access mapping delivered within hours of onset (not days-to-weeks after a state of emergency is declared), covering the ~393 disasters/year that affect ~167 million people/year, so that the first 72 hours of response have usable maps - this is the safety and shelter requirement (EM-DAT 2024).
  • Offline-capable, phone-first local data collection (no persistent high-bandwidth connection, no specialist GIS staff required) so that the last mile of mapping is done by the community that is being mapped - this is the water/health/food/sanitation requirement for the rural and informal-settlement majority.
  • Local-language, community-owned, openly-licensed datasets (ODbL or equivalent) that survive power failures and low-literacy conditions, matching the 'no-tech / low-tech' standard WMO/UNDRR 2023 identifies as essential for the communities at greatest risk - this is the safety and dignity requirement.
  • A locally re-runnable, documented pipeline (not a one-off consultant analysis) so that the dataset updates as settlements, water points and hazard exposure change, at a running cost a district or country can sustain - this is the water/food/sanitation/shelter sustainability requirement that closes the UNOSAT donor-dependence gap.

Constraints

  • Connectivity: 95% of the world's population can access mobile broadband and 88% 4G (WMO/UNDRR 2023) - the solution must work over that infrastructure or over no infrastructure at all; a cloud-only GIS is not acceptable for the target users.
  • Licensing and ownership: data must be open (ODbL or equivalent) and locally owned to be reusable by the governments, NGOs and communities that actually spend the service budget; proprietary basemaps are excluded by the 'open, locally buildable' requirement and by HOT's own positioning.
  • Language and literacy: communities at greatest risk are often multilingual and low-literacy, so the data model and the dissemination layer must support local languages and no-tech channels (WMO/UNDRR 2023).
  • Funding: the only existing free emergency mapping service (UNOSAT-EMS) is donor-funded (Norway) and free only in the emergency phase - a locally buildable solution must not assume a standing donor budget for the operational phase.
  • Scale of the unmapped area: ~170,000 villages at building level is a continental mapping job, not a project; the approach must be volunteer-scale (HOT model: ~700k contributors for ~951M people) plus AI-assisted extraction, not a one-time consultant sweep.
  • Power: the target communities are the same ones WMO/UNDRR 2023 flags as power-failure-vulnerable and low-literacy; hardware and processing must be low-power and field-replaceable.
