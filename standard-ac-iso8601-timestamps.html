One unambiguous time format lets sleep (pyActigraphy), baby (babybuddy), air-quality (AirGradient) and health (OpenSRP) records from different modules align on one timeline across country date formats, at zero cost. RFC 3339 is the free, fully specified profile of ISO 8601 (which is paywalled and allows many forms) that common software parses directly (JSON Schema date-time, PostgreSQL, Python, Home Assistant). Off-grid and village sites often have no internet, so the clock source and the unsynced flag are part of the standard: a wrong timestamp silently corrupts health and sensor records.

Specification

Every emitted or stored data point carries a UTC timestamp in the RFC 3339 profile of ISO 8601-1:2019: YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ, fractional seconds optional (2026-10-07T06:03:41Z, 2026-10-07T06:03:41.250Z). Stored and transmitted times are always UTC with the Z designator, never local time or a numeric offset; local time is for display only. Durations and intervals, where needed, use ISO 8601 duration notation (PT15M). Clock source, in order of preference: NTP (RFC 5905) from the site gateway; the gateway syncs from internet NTP when it has a connection, otherwise from a GNSS receiver or a battery-backed real-time clock (DS3231 class, about 2 ppm). A module that has not synced since power-up marks its records time_synced=false rather than inventing a time. A logger with no clock at all records seconds since boot plus a boot counter, and the gateway assigns UTC when it uploads.
