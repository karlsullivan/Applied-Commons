The need

Road users in low- and middle-income countries (93% of the ~1.19M annual road deaths, leading cause of death for ages 5-29; >50% are pedestrians/cyclists/motorcyclists); workers worldwide (~3M work-related deaths/yr, 395M non-fatal injuries; agriculture, construction, forestry, fishing, manufacturing = 200k fatal injuries/yr, 63% of fatal occupational injuries; Asia-Pacific = 63% of global total); the ~2-billion informal-economy workforce excluded from OSH protection (higher injury rates, no equipment/social protection); women and girls (~840M / 1 in 3 lifetime physical/sexual violence; highest in Oceania 37%, Sub-Saharan Africa 32%, Southern Asia 31%; >1 in 5 ever-partnered girls 15-19 already affected; ~20% sexual violence among refugee women in some conflict settings); children (up to 1 billion aged 2-17 with past-year violence/neglect; homicide a top-4 adolescent cause of death, >80% boys); populations in hazard-prone areas (4.2 billion affected by 7,348 disasters in 2000-2019; ~300 million needing humanitarian assistance in 2024; 123.2 million forcibly displaced at end-2024); and people not covered by early warning (only 119 countries = 60% report a multi-hazard EWS; only 43% of small island developing states; Africa least covered).

Chronic, large-scale, and worsening. Road traffic injuries are the leading cause of death for ages 5-29 and cost most countries ~3% of GDP; the UN target to halve road deaths by 2030 is not on track. Work-related deaths rose >5% versus 2015; the informal sector carries ~2/3 of work-related deaths and disabilities. Violence is essentially stagnant: IPV has declined only 0.2%/yr over 20 years, making SDG target 5.2 very unlikely by 2030. Disasters are accelerating: 7,348 major events 2000-2019 vs 4,212 in 1980-1999, with floods more than doubling (1,389 to 3,254) and climate-related events dominant. Early-warning coverage has improved (119 countries, +113% over a decade; comprehensiveness +45% since 2015) but last-mile and comprehensiveness gaps persist and emerging hazards remain unhandled.

What people do now

Road safety: top-down 'safe system' (safer roads, vehicles, speeds, road users, law enforcement) - national-infrastructure scale, expensive and slow; the most exposed (vulnerable road users) are the least able to buy protection. Occupational safety: national OSH frameworks, labour inspection and PPE that reach the formal sector but exclude the informal economy, where evidence is fragmented and equipment/social protection are absent. Violence prevention: social/institutional (norms change, safe environments, response services, legal reform) with almost no measurable decline (0.2%/yr) and underfunded response services. Hazard protection: national multi-hazard early warning systems, anticipatory action and coordinated humanitarian response plans (2024: 39 plans, 71 countries, 187.8 million people, ~US$48.65 billion) plus the Sendai Framework 2015-2030 (understand risk, governance, invest for resilience, preparedness) - all capital-intensive, reactive and not locally buildable; disaster losses were ~US$2.97 trillion in 2000-2019. Cheap, locally buildable hazard detection exists in the literature (low-cost IoT/sensor landslide and flood early warning on commodity microcontrollers) but is not yet at community scale; WMO notes early warning is a cost-effective tool with a near-tenfold return on investment yet it is underfunded.

Where existing solutions fall short

National multi-hazard early warning: only 60% of countries have one; even where present, last-mile community delivery and comprehensiveness are weak (Africa lowest, small island developing states 43%), and emerging hazards (extreme heat, wildfires, glacial lake outburst floods) are unhandled. Road safety: safe-system solutions are infrastructure/national and expensive; vulnerable road users (over half of deaths) are the least protected and least able to purchase safety. Occupational safety: formal OSH + PPE works, but the informal economy (~2 billion workers; ~2/3 of work-related deaths and disabilities) is excluded - no equipment, no insurance, no inspection. Violence: prevention is social/institutional with almost no measurable progress (0.2%/yr IPV decline) and underfunded response services; no low-cost, locally deployable detection/protection equivalent exists for interpersonal violence. Personal protective equipment: affordable, standard-compliant PPE is scarce for the most exposed (informal workers, vulnerable road users, displaced populations) and the governed supply chain is slow and costly, leaving room for local production. Cost asymmetry: reconstruction and response are capital-intensive (~US$2.97T losses 2000-2019; ~US$48.65B/yr for 187.8M people), while WMO reports early warning delivers ~10x ROI yet is underfunded - the unmet, locally addressable gap is cheap, early, local hazard detection plus personal protection rather than large infrastructure.

What a solution must do

  • Multi-hazard detection: one node detects at least floods + landslides plus one more hazard (e.g. extreme heat or storm) and issues an evacuation-ready alert with lead time >= 30 minutes for flash-flood/landslide and >= 24 h for fluvial flood.
  • Cost: <= US$50 in materials per sensing/alert node (commodity MCU + off-the-shelf sensors + radio), so a 1,000-node community network costs <= US$50,000 in hardware.
  • Power: >= 30 days of operation on a single battery/USB recharge or a <= 10 W solar panel; no mains power assumed.
  • Connectivity: functions with no cellular/Internet (offline radio mesh or SMS/USSD fallback); monthly connectivity cost <= US$10 or zero.
  • Actionability: one node raises a human-actionable warning (audible siren + SMS/visual) within 5 minutes of crossing its trigger threshold (e.g. river gauge +/-5%, soil-moisture/slope-displacement threshold).
  • Accuracy (measured against a reference over a defined wet season): true-positive alert rate >= 90% and false-alarm rate <= 10%.
  • Maintainability: a trained local community member (no engineer) can calibrate, replace and interpret the node in <= 30 minutes using a printed local-language runbook.
  • Openness: hardware, firmware and data schema fully open-source with a publicly reproducible bill of materials on a commodity MCU (ESP32/Arduino-class), no vendor lock-in.
  • Personal PPE variant (where applicable): meets a named published standard (e.g. EN 166 face shield, EN 397 hard hat, ISO 14126) and is reproducible from a stated materials cost.
  • Reproducibility: a volunteer with a soldering iron or 3D printer can build the unit in one working session (<= 4 hours) from an open build pack.

Constraints

  • Power: unreliable or absent mains; solar/battery only, with cold-weather battery derating.
  • Connectivity: low/intermittent mobile coverage and expensive bandwidth; must operate offline.
  • Tools & skills: local builders without engineering training; limited to soldering, 3D printing and basic assembly; no precision instrumentation.
  • Materials: commodity and locally sourceable or shippable in volume; must survive the hazard they monitor (humidity, heat, dust, UV, flooding of the install site).
  • Climate: tropical heat and rain, flood-prone siting, dust, salt air.
  • Maintenance: no onsite engineers; spare parts locally obtainable; self-diagnostic.
  • Language & trust: warnings must be actionable in the local language for non-literate users (siren, pictograms) and must overcome 'cry wolf' via community co-design (per UNDRR: systems co-developed with local communities).
  • Cost ceiling: per-node <= ~US$50 to stay fundable against capital-intensive national multi-hazard early-warning systems and response plans.
