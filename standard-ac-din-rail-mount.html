The worldwide standard for mounting breakers, relays, PLCs and controllers in distribution boards; steel rail costs under about $1/m and is stocked by every electrical wholesaler, so any module mounts in any enclosure. Citing the current IEC 60715 edition and DIN 43880 dimensions makes modules fit off-the-shelf enclosures and covers. Keeping extra-low voltage apart from mains on shared boards is the main safety point.

Specification

Top-hat rail TH35 per IEC 60715:2017 (EN 60715, which replaced EN 50022): 35 x 7.5 mm for control electronics, 35 x 15 mm where heavier devices hang. Module housings follow the modular-device dimensions of DIN 43880: widths in whole modules (17.5 mm nominal, 18 mm pitch; 1M, 2M, 4M = 72 mm) and a 45 mm front profile, so modules fit standard distribution-board and consumer-unit covers. Click-on mounting with a release clip, no screws; an end stop at each end of every row. Rail of zinc-plated or stainless steel; aluminium allowed indoors. DIN-rail modules sit in an enclosure meeting ac-ip44-enclosure. An enclosure that also holds mains devices is a low-voltage assembly: it follows IEC 61439-3 (distribution boards operated by ordinary persons) or the local equivalent, and its mains wiring is done by a qualified person under the local wiring rules (IEC 60364 or the national code). Extra-low-voltage modules are kept separate from mains circuits as SELV and PELV require (IEC 60364-4-41): on a separate rail or behind a barrier, with their wiring segregated from mains wiring.
