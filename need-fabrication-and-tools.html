The need

The people whose essential needs depend on working, repairable equipment are the majority of the global population living in low- and middle-income settings. Water: 2.2 billion people lacked safely managed drinking water in 2022, incl. 292 million with limited supply (>30 min collection) and 296 million drawing from unprotected wells/springs (WHO). Sanitation: 1.5 billion lack basic sanitation and 419 million defecate in the open (WHO). Energy: 730 million lacked electricity in 2024 (IEA) and 655 million without electricity plus 2 billion on polluting cooking fuels (WHO/SDG7); off-grid solar and mini-grids already serve hundreds of millions. Health: hospitals/clinics depend on maintainable equipment (ventilator-maintenance failure in 2020). Sub-Saharan Africa carries the largest shares — 8 of 10 people without electricity; 560 million+ without electricity and 970 million without clean cooking (IEA/WHO).

Severe and persistent. Failure/absence of working equipment underlies ~505,000 diarrhoeal deaths/yr from contaminated water, ~564,000/yr from unsafe sanitation, and ~1.4 million/yr from inadequate water-sanitation-hygiene combined — overwhelmingly in low- and middle-income countries. Electrification progress must roughly triple to reach universal access by 2030, and most of the remaining gap is in sub-Saharan Africa. Where machines delivering water, sanitation, power and care cannot be made or repaired locally, those needs stay unmet and the harm compounds.

What people do now

Repair and manufacturing of essential equipment is largely a 'repair monopoly': the maker controls parts, manuals and diagnostic tools, so independent repair is difficult or prohibitively expensive. Repair monopolies are linked to higher prices, longer delays, reduced serviceability and less choice; more than half the population of the western world has a used/broken device at home that is not economically repairable. In poor, remote communities a broken pump, farm implement or medical device therefore stays broken: the cost is lost water, food or care, or a trip to buy an unaffordable replacement.

Where existing solutions fall short

Most essential machines are sold as closed systems whose maker owns repair, so poor and remote communities effectively cannot repair what they need and instead buy new or let it fail; the repair economy is fragmented, under-resourced and concentrated where incomes are highest, leaving the regions with the largest unmet need least served; medical-equipment maintenance gaps were starkly exposed in 2020 and persist; and there is no widely adopted, standardized, open and locally buildable set of workshop machines and repair processes specifically oriented to the spares and assets that keep water, sanitation, food, energy and health working.

What a solution must do

  • Water/sanitation: fabricate and repair the spares that keep water points functional — pumps, pipe fittings, taps, storage tanks, filtration housings and latrine components — so a failed supply point is restored locally, not waited on externally.
  • Food: repair and maintain small farm implements, hand/low-power tools and small irrigation pumps so smallholders are not forced to buy new equipment when one breaks.
  • Health: open repair access for common clinic/hospital equipment (diagnostics and life-support devices) so a fault is fixed locally and fast, not queued for the manufacturer.
  • Energy: open, locally buildable assembly and repair for off-grid/mini-grid components (inverter, charge controller, battery mounting, cabling) so off-grid power that serves hundreds of millions stays available.
  • Shelter/construction: locally available, replicable fabrication (cutting, forming, joining) for building materials and components.
  • Cross-cutting: designs/processes open (parts, drawings, instructions freely available), using locally obtainable inputs, serviceable by a trained non-specialist, and affordable so repair costs less than the replacement it avoids.

Constraints

  • Dominant barrier is the repair monopoly — restrictions on parts, manuals, tools and diagnostic software that block independent repair.
  • Off-grid/remote settings require the solution to be self-contained, transportable and operable without specialist parts logistics.
  • Skilled labour is scarce where need is greatest; processes must be learnable and safe for a trained local operator, not an OEM technician.
  • Materials and inputs must be locally procurable and affordable.
