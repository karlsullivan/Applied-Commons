Used by6 modules

40 mm square tube is stocked by almost every steel supplier and workshop and can be cut, drilled and welded with basic tools, in Europe and in low-income countries. Putting the 100 mm grid on one common member makes modules and frames interchangeable. Maintainer decision needed: the member size is a design choice; confirm or change it before approving.

Specification

Frames that carry modules are built from square steel hollow section 40 x 40 mm with a 2 mm wall (cold-formed, EN 10219-1 and EN 10219-2, grade S235JRH or better), with M6 clearance holes (6.6 mm, ac-iso-metric-fasteners) on the 100 mm grid along the centre line, so any module plate bolts to any frame. Joints are welded, or bolted through gusset plates. Outdoor frames are hot-dip galvanised to ISO 1461 after drilling and welding, or painted where galvanising is not available. Light indoor frames may use 40 x 40 mm aluminium slot extrusion with fixing plates drilled on the same 100 mm grid. Timber frames (strength-graded structural timber per EN 338 / EN 14081, for example C24 45 x 95 mm) are allowed for sheds and housings, with modules fixed through plates. Each frame states its load rating, checked by calculation in the build pack or by a proof load of 1.5 times the rating. Heavier structures (towers, roofs, tank stands) are designed individually and are outside this standard.
