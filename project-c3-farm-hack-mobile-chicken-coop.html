Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • The named FarmHack 'mobile chicken coop' (Sunny Slope Orchard, published 2013-12-20) is a 5'x10' pen-on-wheels with an enclosed roosting/nest area, a steel lever (1"x2" channel iron) that lifts the wheeled coop 4" for moving, and a 12V automatic door driven by a used Honda Civic power-window motor, a gate-opener battery, an Amico CN101A timer, a DPDT relay, and a 1/2W solar panel. The build log states the frame is 2x2 redwood and plywood. It reports NO total build cost and NO build time; the author only says it was built 'about a year ago.'
  • The lift is a welded crank: 5/8-inch steel spindles welded into 1x2-inch channel iron (a ~4-foot lever arm vs a ~6-inch short side) on a 5/8-inch shaft offset 4 inches from the wheel spindles; rotating the lever ~90 degrees raises the coop ~4 inches. Note the question's '5/8 inch offset shaft' conflates the shaft/spindle diameter (5/8 inch) with the offset that sets lift height (4 inches).
  • The project's own framing material is Pacific redwood, which is exactly the premium wood the question excludes; therefore the published project cannot answer the low-income local-wood question and the cost/time must be re-estimated for a substitute stock (local pine/pine-2x4, fence boards, pallets, plywood scrap).
  • Data the sources do NOT provide (honest gaps): no retrieved source gives a low-income, regional, or non-US price for local wood or salvage stock - all cost figures are US-retail ballparks - and no source gives a store/grocery egg price, so the store-vs-homemade egg-arbitrage comparison is left to the reader's local egg price. Confidence in any low-income-context cost is therefore capped by the absence of regional pricing.
  • The Farm Hack mobile chicken coop is documented with photos, video and prose only; no dimensioned drawings, cut list, or bill of materials were published on Farm Hack.
  • The original builder (Sunny Slope Orchard) confirmed in the build blog that no detailed plans exist - 'I do not have detailed plans. I used mostly scrap materials so the design was partly shaped by what I had on hand' - so converting to published dimensioned drawings requires new reverse-engineering from the photos and text, not locating existing drawings.
  • In a low-income salvage/local-wood context, a mobile coop/tractor for 4-6 hens with an enclosed roost end and run costs roughly $120-350 in materials. Backyardsteadlab puts a 4x8 tractor at $120-350 by frame type; momdeco reports builders who use reclaimed lumber (fence boards, old shed siding, pallet wood) 'consistently land closer to $150 to $250 total' vs $350-450 all-new; a basic pallet-frame build is ~$180 in materials with pallets often obtained free from local businesses.
  • Build time for the salvage-class mobile coop is ~6-16 hours for a simple frame (pallet/hoop 4-10 h; 2x2/2x4 A-frame ark 10-16 h) and ~15-25 hours, i.e. 2-4 weekends, for a fully predator-proofed coop with roosts, a nest box, hardware-cloth on all openings, and a buried/bent predator skirt; a one-person full-time build can finish the structure in 2-3 days.
