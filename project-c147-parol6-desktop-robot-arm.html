PAROL6 is a 3D-printed six-axis desktop robot arm by Source Robotics, built to resemble an industrial arm in its mechanics, control software and usability. Its STL files, control software and GUI are open source under GPL-3.0; kits and assembled arms are also sold. It suits teaching automation and light pick-and-place work in a small workshop.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Spec envelope (vendor docs, page 2_2): 6 DOF; 400 mm reach; payload 1 kg near the base, 0.5 kg across the full workspace; PETG structure; open-loop stepper drivers with limit switches; 2 isolated outputs, 1 CAN bus, USB. A fabrication head must deliver force well beyond the 0.5 kg full-workspace payload rating, so the arm is under-rated for the task.
  • The complete part list is published and authoritative in the official repo's BOM.md (mechanical, electrical, printed, and end-effector sections), so a low-resource workshop has a single canonical bill of materials to buy against rather than reverse-engineering.
  • Print-bed constraint: only two printed parts do not fit a bed smaller than ~175 mm - Upper_arm (smallest containing cube 160.5 mm, 220 g, 18 h print) and J1_turret_rework_blocker (154.1 mm) - and no rotation makes them fit. Every other part prints on a smaller bed.
  • An official small-print-bed modification exists (STL/SMALL_PRINT_BED): it splits Upper_arm and J1_turret_rework_blocker each into two halves joined with steel dowel pins + two-part epoxy, leaving every other part unchanged; it adds 3x dowel pin 4x18 mm, 4x dowel pin 6x22 mm, and 1x 1:1 epoxy to the BOM. The docs warn a single moulded part is always stronger than a bonded one, so use the mod only when the standard parts genuinely do not fit.
  • The motors are six NEMA17 steppers in three torque classes: 1x 16 Ncm (J1 base), 3x 45 Ncm, and 2x 65 Ncm, each paired with an EG17 gearbox - not six identical motors.
  • No drilling, cutting, or forming end-effector has been demonstrated or documented for PAROL6 in any public source. The full recursive open-source repository tree (3.2 MB, all files) contains zero file or path matches for drill/cut/spindle/grind/saw/weld/solder/tap/lathe/mill/end-effector/toolhead; the only end-effector directory is STL/GRIPPER_ATTACHMENTS holding a pneumatic gripper and a vacuum gripper holder. The community-projects list shows only vision-guided, teleoperation, PCB-testing and print/mod builds; the Hackaday log index enumerates AI assembly tooling, PCB testing, LEAP teleop, guides, vacuum gripper and forum - none a fabrication head; and a web search for '"PAROL6" drill OR drilling OR cutting OR spindle OR grinder OR saw' returns zero results.
  • Structural/duty-cycle reliability is explicitly bounded by the vendor: when using the robot for longer periods, currents must be reduced 'or you risk destroying your robot' (docs page 2_2). The device is a precision positioning/education arm, not a machine tool.
  • Gearbox, belt, and switch availability is a non-issue for a low-resource shop: the BOM specifies 3x EG17 precision planetary gearboxes (20:1, 20:1, 10:1) with an MG-series (1:5/1:10/1:20) cheaper alternative, 4x HTD3M belts (lengths 396/342/201/246) with 12T and 15T pulleys, 3x ZW12-3 limit switches, and 3x inductive end-of-travel sensors - all commodity parts.
