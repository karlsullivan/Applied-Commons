About Applied Commons

From Applied Commons, the open catalogue of ways to meet human needs

Applied Commons catalogues open solutions to human needs and, for the most promising, writes complete build packs: parts lists, design files, assembly and test procedures. It is organised by need, from water, food and shelter to learning and community, with a separate group of enablers, such as energy and fabrication, that every need relies on.

How it is made

Research agents (language models running on Applied Commons' own hardware) find candidate projects, screen them against each need, research open questions from public sources, and draft build packs, safety reviews and standards. A person reviews and approves standards and capability maps before they are published as approved. Nothing is described as built or tested until it has been.

Every claim should trace to a source. Where the evidence is missing, the article says so.

Prices are in euros. A build pack priced in another currency is converted at the European Central Bank's reference rate on the day the site was built, with the original figure beside it. Prices are estimates from public suppliers, not quotes.

Licences

  • Text on this site: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0.
  • Applied Commons hardware designs: CERN-OHL-S-2.0. Software: GPL-3.0-or-later.
  • Linked projects keep their own licences, shown in each article. Applied Commons builds on open and share-alike designs wherever possible.
  • Non-commercial marks a non-commercial licence, such as CC BY-NC: the design may be copied and changed, but not sold or used commercially, and the same holds for anything made from it. Applied Commons is non-commercial, so it keeps and links such designs, always marked. It uses them as references first, and builds on one only when no open design serves; what it builds then keeps the non-commercial licence and is marked the same way.
  • Sources are cited, not copied, unless their licence allows it. So that designs are not lost when a site goes away, every design source is also kept by a public archive: repositories by Software Heritage, files and pages by the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. Files whose licence allows copying and changes are also kept by Applied Commons itself, as releases of its public repository; non-commercial ones are marked there too. Each article links to those copies.

Safety

Build packs document existing designs from public material. They are not evidence that a build is safe or performs as claimed. A safety review lists the hazards and controls it finds, but it is not a certification, and nothing here is professional engineering, medical or legal advice. Follow local regulations, and use a qualified person where an article says so.
