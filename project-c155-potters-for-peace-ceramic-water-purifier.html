Program (since 1998) for a low-tech, low-cost colloidal-silver ceramic water purifier (CWP) made from local terra-cotta clay + sawdust and fired locally; ~$15-25 per unit, $4-6 per replacement filter, ~99.88% reduction of water-borne disease agents, produced at 50+ independent factories in 30+ countries as locally-owned businesses. Technology shared openly under an open-source manufacturing model with no patent held (ICAITI/Guatemala 1981 design). Meets local-sourcing, no-infrastructure, cost-floor, and maintenance-with-local-tools constraints. Mature and widely deployed.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Lantagne (2001, USAID/Alethia Environmental) explicitly leaves the effect of repeated scrubbing on the colloidal silver layer unresolved: he notes the silver sorbs into the ceramic to a depth greater than the microlayer removed by brushing, but states 'this indication has not been tested' and recommends (but does not report) a test in which a silvered filter is scrubbed to approximate monthly scrubbings for one year (Report 2) / 24 monthly scrubbings (Report 1) and retested. No public dataset measuring silver concentration or microbial inactivation against a scrubbing-cycle count was found in any PFP, UNICEF, or university report retrieved.
  • The '1-2 years' replacement interval in Smart Disinfection Solutions (2010, IWA S2Small) is about partial COMPONENT replacement, not silver: for the PFP CWP it states 'the estimated lifespan is up to 5 years, but normally partial replacement (cracks in filter element or bottom container, broken tap) of the system will occur within 1 or 2 years'; cleaning is 'scrub the surface of the ceramic filter with a sturdy brush and flush with water', with pre-sedimentation if input is highly turbid.
  • Water-logging / watertightness QC gate - named and defined in the standard: CMWG 2011 Sec 8.3.3 'Pressure Testing' (Fig 8-1 'Pressure Test and Evidence of Leaks', Pillsers & Diaz 2009): hold filter by rim, submerge the base in water to near the rim, keep submerged 10 s; if evidence of water entering (esp. unevenly) after 10 s, the filter has cracks or large pores and 'must be destroyed'; alternate method = invert rim-down fully submerged, a stream of bubbles from wall/base = destroy. Table 11 assigns this test to 'each filter' (100% of finished elements), done before silver application.
  • Documented re-impregnation protocol (PFP standard): 2 mL of 3.2% industrial-concentration colloidal silver (Microdyn) diluted in 250 mL of filtered water, brushed onto BOTH the inside and outside of the fired filter element; the dose was 1 mL before 2000 and doubled to 2 mL in 2000 for an extra safety margin. Naturopathic-grade US silver was found ineffective and is not to be used.
  • Documented reapplication interval: PFP recommends reapplying colloidal silver once per year; Lantagne's 2001 lab data (filters 2 and 7 years old still removing 100% of total and fecal coliform) indicated this 'does not seem to be necessary' but is retained as a field safety factor that 'should not be removed without more extensive testing.'
  • Silver does not measurably 'wear out' over time: a 7-year-old PFP filter (CIRA-UNAN challenge, Dec 1999) removed 100% of total and fecal coliform; Campbell 2005 (via Donachy) found 17 of 19 field elements aged 1-6 years removed 100% of E. coli and total coliform (all 5-year-old elements 100%; a 6-year-old 100% E. coli, 94% total coliform) and revised the element life span from the previously recommended 2 years to up to 5 years.
  • Field longevity corroborates the silver's durability: Brown & Sobsey (2007, UNC, Cambodia, filters in use 2-4 years) found no strong correlation between filter effectiveness and time in use and concluded filters 'can maintain effectiveness for up to 4 years (and potentially longer)', with the main failure mode being physical breakage (average time in use before disuse ~2 years, ~2% of filters falling into disuse per month, largely from breakage).
  • What the evidence does NOT establish: there is no retrieved measurement of how the antimicrobial (silver) layer specifically degrades as a function of repeated user scrubbing cycles under field conditions; the 2- and 7-year and 2-4-year effectiveness data confirm the silver persists, but the scrubbing-specific wear test Lantagne called for is absent from the public record. Filter-level limiting factors reported are decreasing flow rate (clogging) and physical breakage, not silver loss.
