Free, open-source (GPL-3.0, 479 stars) software framework for climate risk assessment and adaptation-option appraisal (CLIMADA-project, ETH Zurich). Quantifies hazard impact and compares adaptation measures, supporting the brief's documented benefit-cost (BCR) requirement for coastal and climate adaptation. Climate-independent software, runs year-round in cold or warm regions.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • CLIMADA's core tropical-cyclone hazard (climada_python) models WIND only (Holland 2008 / Emanuel parametric wind fields from IBTrACS tracks); it has no physical storm-surge component. The 2019 GMD platform paper lists coupled rainfall+surge modelling as explicit future work.
  • Storm surge in CLIMADA is an optional add-on in the separate climada_petals package with two implementations: TCSurgeBathtub, a crude linear wind-to-surge proxy (surge[m] = 0.1023*(v-26.82)+1.8288, Xu 2010/SLOSH-based, 0.2 m/km inland decay per Pielke 1997), and TCSurgeGeoClaw, a full 2D shallow-water flow solver (up to ~270 m resolution, HPC-scale, Linux/Mac only).
  • Wind-based TC economic-damage calibration (Eberenz et al. 2021, NHESS; 473 EM-DAT matched events, 1980-2017, 53 countries): the uncalibrated default impact function biases damage by up to a factor of 36 (N-W Pacific overestimation) and factor 5 (S Indian Ocean underestimation); regional calibration brings total damage ratios to ~1 per region, but single-event damage ratios still span several orders of magnitude (post-calibration RMSF ~6-36). The authors conclude the setup is 'not suitable to represent single TC events adequately.'
  • CLIMADA outputs a directly usable per-measure benefit-cost ratio: CostBenefit.calc() returns per-measure cost, averted-damage benefit, and Benefit/Cost ratio (plus a per-measure dict, present and future scenarios, discount rates, and a combined-measures view).
  • CLIMADA contains NO physical mangrove/marsh attenuation or averted-damage function. Zero code hits for mangrove/marsh/vegetation across the full file trees of climada_python, climada_petals and climada_papers. The 'Mangrove' in the MeasureSet tutorial is a placeholder adaptation measure with an arbitrary flat hazard-intensity impact (e.g. -10), not a physical averted-damage model.
  • The GeoClaw flow solver that CLIMADA's TCSurgeGeoClaw wraps was validated (Vogt et al. 2024, Communications Earth & Environment) against satellite-based floodplain observations for a global set of 71 storms; the models overall have 'only moderate skill in reproducing the observed floodplains.' GeoClaw outperforms the two static (incl. wind->inundation/bathtub-style) approaches but over-predicts inundation (true-negative-rate 93% vs 97-99% for the static models), and a full-scale ocean model (GTSMM) reproduces tide-gauge hydrographs better than GeoClaw.
  • Because CLIMADA's TC damage is driven by wind speed only, surge- and rain-driven damage is absorbed implicitly into the calibrated wind-based impact function and is poorly represented for surge-dominated, low-wind events. The Philippines case study in Eberenz et al. 2021 shows Tropical Storm Ketsana (2009, flood/surge-driven, low wind) with an event-damage ratio of 0.002 (simulated damage >2 orders of magnitude below reported) and Typhoon Haiyan at 0.17.
  • CLIMADA can incorporate nature-based protection costs: a Measure is a user-supplied scalar cost in the same currency units as the assets plus linear hazard/impact modifiers (hazard_inten_imp, paa_impact, mdd_impact); the official CLIMADA docs use a 'Mangrove' as a worked example measure, so a mangrove US$/ha figure can be converted to a total cost and plugged in as a measure (a cost curve as several discrete measures).
