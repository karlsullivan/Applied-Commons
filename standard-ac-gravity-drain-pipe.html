Sanitation, biogas and handwashing modules all need gravity drains, which the pressure-pipe standard does not cover; module design proposed four separate port specs for them. PVC-U and PP drain pipe in 32-110 mm sizes is sold almost everywhere and push-fit joints need no tools. The falls, access and no-traps rules prevent the usual failure, blockage, and the discharge and frost rules cover the health and cold-climate risks.

Specification

Gravity (non-pressure) drains and waste lines between modules use PVC-U or PP pipe sized by outside diameter: 32, 40 or 50 mm for waste water and greywater (EN 1329-1 PVC-U or EN 1451-1 PP, or the local equivalent); 110 mm for slurry, sewage and digestate (EN 1401-1 PVC-U, SN 4 minimum when buried, or EN 13476 structured-wall pipe). Joints are push-fit with elastomeric ring seals (EN 681-1) so lines can be taken apart; solvent weld only for joints that stay permanent. Falls of at least 1:50 for 32-50 mm and 1:80 for 110 mm, installed per EN 12056 inside buildings and EN 752 outside, with a rodding or cleaning access at every change of direction. Urine lines: as short and steep as possible, at least 1:50 (urine-diversion guidance sets 1 % as the minimum), no sharp 90-degree bends, no low points or traps that hold urine (struvite and other deposits block them), and a sealed vent. At least 50 mm bore where the run is steep and easy to clean, and 75 mm or more for long or shallow runs or where access is difficult; flexible hose only for a short, accessible final connection. Biogas digesters: 110 mm slurry inlet; digestate outlet of at least 50 mm, placed so it sets the liquid level and keeps the gas space sealed. Cold climates: buried below the local frost depth or insulated, and laid to drain fully when not in use. Untreated sewage, urine or digestate never discharges to surface water; outlets go to treatment, storage or a soakaway under the local rules. Connections to pressure lines use ac-fluid-dn-threads.
