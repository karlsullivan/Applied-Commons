LibreMesh turns ordinary OpenWrt routers into a self-configuring community network that villages build and run themselves, with no central point to fail. It gives every other module a local IP transport where commercial backhaul does not reach.

Specification

Community IP networks are built from OpenWrt routers running LibreMesh, which meshes nodes automatically with BATMAN-adv at layer 2 and a layer-3 routing protocol (BMX6 or Babel, depending on the LibreMesh release); every node shares the community profile (channel, mesh ID, keys, addressing), with no central controller. Each node may offer a client hotspot. Links between mesh nodes are encrypted with a community key (WPA2 or WPA3), even where the hotspot is open. Radio: in Europe 2.4 GHz within ETSI EN 300 328 and 5 GHz within ETSI EN 301 893. In the EU, 5150-5350 MHz is indoor only, so outdoor links use 5470-5725 MHz with DFS and TPC. Elsewhere, the national equivalents apply. Radio equipment placed on the EU market meets the RED cybersecurity requirements (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, EN 18031-1): unique admin passwords, security updates, no management interface open to clients. Shared internet goes out through a gateway node whose owner has agreed the terms with their provider. Uplinks to a LoRa gateway or a cellular node are optional. The mesh carries MQTT (ac-mqtt-iot-bus) and local services.
