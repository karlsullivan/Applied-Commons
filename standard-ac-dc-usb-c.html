USB-C 5 V power input for low-draw modules

USB-C 5 V power input for low-draw modules
Codeac-dc-usb-c
Kindelectrical
StatusApproved
ApprovedOctober 2026

One reversible connector replaces micro-USB, mini-USB and proprietary plugs; mandated on EU consumer electronics since late 2024, so any module can be powered from a phone charger or power bank anywhere at cents per connector. Two 5.1 kOhm resistors are the whole sink circuit at 5 V: no PD controller, and the most common real fault (missing Rd) is designed out. Staying at or below 1.5 A means it works from almost any charger. A compliant charger never raises the voltage unless the module negotiates it, so the protection guards against faulty equipment, at a cost of well under a dollar.

Specification

Connector: USB Type-C receptacle per IEC 62680-1-3:2026 (USB Type-C Cable and Connector Specification Release 2.4); the module is a power sink. Each CC pin has its own 5.1 kOhm pull-down (Rd) to ground. This is mandatory: boards that omit Rd or tie the CC pins together get no power from USB-C to USB-C chargers. No USB Power Delivery negotiation (IEC 62680-1-2): a sink that does not negotiate receives only the default 5 V (vSafe5V, 4.75-5.5 V). Current: at most 1.5 A (7.5 W) continuous, so any Type-C source advertising 1.5 A or 3.0 A, and USB-A chargers through an A-to-C cable, can power it; a module may draw up to 3.0 A only after reading a 3.0 A advertisement on CC. Larger loads use ac-poe-power-data or the DC bus. Input protection: a fuse (resettable or one-shot) sized to the module's draw, a TVS diode for surges, and an overvoltage cut-off (load switch or OVP IC tripping by about 6 V), so a faulty or non-compliant charger or cable cannot damage the module. Data lines optional (USB 2.0, for firmware updates). Safety basis: a 5 V input of 15 W or less is ES1 and PS1 under IEC 62368-1:2023 (EN IEC 62368-1, AS/NZS 62368.1, UL 62368-1); modules are designed to it. Supply from any phone charger or power bank that complies with IEC 62368-1.
