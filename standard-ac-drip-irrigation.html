Drip irrigation laterals and emitters

ApprovedOctober 2026

Drip irrigation is the most water-efficient way to grow food and scales from a gravity kit for a family garden to a farm. 16 mm laterals and ISO 9261 emitters are sold everywhere, so parts are interchangeable; filtering and flushing are what keep drip systems working.

Specification

Drip irrigation uses 16 mm outside-diameter polyethylene laterals (the size sold worldwide), with emitters and emitting pipe meeting ISO 9261 and carrying the manufacturer data it requires (nominal flow, pressure range, uniformity class). Pressure-compensating emitters where the field is not level or runs are long; non-compensating ones on short, level runs. Low-head gravity kits (a tank about 1 to 2 m above the field) use emitters or drip tape rated for low pressure. Every system has a filter before the laterals as fine as the emitter maker requires (commonly 120 mesh, about 130 um), a flush valve or open end at the end of each lateral, and a pressure regulator where the supply exceeds the emitters' range. Mainlines and sub-mains follow ac-fluid-dn-threads (PE pipe, BSP fittings); connections between 16 mm laterals and the sub-main use barbed or compression fittings made for 16 mm pipe. Fertiliser is injected downstream of a backflow preventer so nothing can flow back into a drinking-water supply (EN 1717). Controller-driven valves use ac-modbus-control or a local controller, and fail closed.
