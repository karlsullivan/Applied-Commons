Purdue Improved Crop Storage (PICS)

From Applied Commons, the open catalogue of ways to meet human needs
Purdue Improved Crop Storage (PICS)
NeedCold chain and storage
ServesFood and nutrition, Economic and livelihood security, Environmental stability and resource security
StatusBuild pack ready
LicenceNone found
Climatestropical, arid, temperate, cold
Parts costabout €1.64 (1.80 USD)
SafetyControls needed
The safety review found hazards that need controls when building or using this design.

Hermetic grain storage: a cost-effective triple-layer bag (two HDPE inner layers + woven polypropylene outer) that stores shelled grain (maize, sorghum, wheat, rice, etc.) 6-12 months without fumigation, at ~$2.50 per 50 kg. Gates-funded PICS3 is field-deployed across West and East Africa; public Purdue extension PDFs document the design. Field-proven, low-cost, locally sourced - the direct match to the airtight/dry grain requirement. Requires shelled grain and closing labour, a known adoption constraint the brief notes.

Failure modes

Rodent intrusion (mice/rats)Total or near-total loss of grain - PICS bags do NOT protect against rodentsStore bags raised on pallets/shelves, in a rodent-proof sealed structure, away from walls; add physical rodent control around the storage point

Known gaps

What the original design does not document:

  • No per-part engineering BOM is published upstream (exact film area/weight per 50 kg liner, part numbers, closure-tie spec and count) - component-level unit costs left null rather than guessed
  • Component-level per-part USD costs in West/East Africa not re-verified this run; the only historical figure (Mali 2010: ~500 FCFA liner pair, ~51% of factory price; factory delivery 925 FCFA) is from a prior run and not re-cited
  • No current (2025-26) single-bag in-country retail price was retrieved; the 2012 Burkina Faso price and the 2026 USD 2-3 largest-size estimate are the closest anchors
  • Exact moisture threshold and 6-12 month storage claim are stated across sources but the per-crop duration was not isolated from a single source this run
  • No CAD / parametric model, blueprint, or fabrication repository exists for the PICS hermetic bag — confirmed by GitHub repo search (0 results across 'pics hermetic grain storage', 'pics bag storage', 'hermetic grain bag', 'purdue improved crop storage', and sheller variants; no Purdue org hosts a PICS design repo). The bag must be fabricated to spec from the extension PDFs; a builder wanting a parametric model must obtain it from Purdue Cal's directly or draft their own from the dimensioned pages.
  • No standalone dimensioned drawing file exists: the full bag-construction geometry (triple-layer layering, 50/100 kg sizes, lip-and-knot sealing) is carried only inside the E-265-W PDF and the PICS3 poster, not as a separate CAD/drawing file.
  • Exact author/title of the 2018 JSPR efficacy paper (PII S0022474X18301371) could not be fully verified: ScienceDirect and the publisher landing page bot-blocked metadata extraction, so it is indexed by journal + year + PII + host folder rather than full author/title; confirm against the first page before relying on it for the efficacy numbers.
  • Hand-maize-sheller cut-list provenance: the dimensioned cut list is attributed to Appropedia / Engineers Without Borders (appropedia.org); that upstream source should be confirmed before mass reproduction if exact tolerances matter (18-22 mm drum-housing clearance).
  • PICS is ambient hermetic storage with no temperature control or monitoring, so no design for a monitoring sub-system is in scope; if the broader build-pack assumes a sensor/monitoring component, that is outside the PICS bag as designed (health cold-chain fit = MISSED per the input fit brief).
  • No published bag-level hermeticity / airtightness test standard (ASABE / ISO / CIGR) specific to PICS bags was found in the retrieved sources; published protocols are ad-hoc O2-indicator (Oxysense/Oxydot) and USB data-logger methods, so a standardized O2 pass/fail threshold for the bag itself is undefined
  • Field (smallholder) validation of the full 6-12 month MAIZE window is thinner than the cowpea window - most published efficacy is cowpea plus medium/short-term maize lab studies; the 6-12 month maize claim leans on the need brief and the 692-household Nepal RCT
  • The ~10 kg/50-kg-bag (~1/3 baseline) loss-reduction figure is the need brief's causal estimate (Nepal RCT, 692 households, ~44 kg/household/season baseline); the full RCT paper body was not fully extracted here, so its exact estimator/CI could not be independently re-derived
  • No standardized aflatoxin / mold acceptance threshold tied to PICS was located in the retrieved sources
  • Rodent protection is out of scope for the bag unit itself (structure-level test) and is not documented for PICS
  • Reuse limit (>=3 seasons) is extension/industry guidance, not a documented pass/fail durability test

Safety

Safe to build with the listed controls; no qualified-person step required. The bag itself is a passive, non-pressurized, chemical-free, ambient hermetic store. Add two controls not in the pack: (1) a drum/finger guard + eye protection + no-gloves + keep-children-away rule for the hand-cranked maize sheller (required pre-step); (2) a two-person lift or trolley and waist-height pallet/shelf storage for 50-100 kg bags. The pack already carries the critical food-safety controls (seal at <=13-14% m.c. verified by moisture tester; test for aflatoxin/mycotoxin at open; confirm no fumigant/chemical; rodent-protected raised storage).

HazardSeverityControls
Grain sealed above the safe-moisture band (18-24% m.c.) and held 6-12 months: mold growth, fermentation, and aflatoxin production by Aspergillus. Aflatoxin is a potent carcinogen that is especially dangerous to children; the pack's own data document ~95.5-percentage-point germination loss at 24% m.c. over 21 days and visible mold at 21-24% m.c. in non-hermetic PP bags. Grain stored after fumigation or co-stored with fumigated/insecticide-treated grain can carry pesticide residues. Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Yewle 2024 (Foods 13(5):760) is a controlled growth-chamber study (25 C, 80% RH, 21 days), not a field trial: PICS bags preserved 18% and 21% m.c. maize at constant moisture with no visible mold (O2 fell below 1%), with germination losses of 0.5 and 6.2 percentage points, while 24% m.c. maize failed even in hermetic bags (95.5 pp germination loss, lactic-acid fermentation, pH drop); the authors' own verdict is that <=21% m.c. for 21 days is the safe boundary and 'field testing is required' before farm deployment.
  • The closing step of the conventional PICS triple-bag is explicitly documented by its own developers as a labour burden: bags are closed by twisting, folding and tying the lip of each of the three layers individually with cord, which is 'not only time and labor intensive, but also may affect the integrity of the liners' (Kharel et al. 2018, J Stored Prod Res).
  • A redesigned closure demonstrably cuts sealing time: in a controlled comparison, fold-and-tie (both liners folded together and tied) was the most time-saving method, reducing 50 kg PICS bag sealing time by more than 34% versus the conventional twist-tie method, with no difference in oxygen-depletion rate or pest suppression (Kharel et al. 2018).
  • Scope/verification caveat: the cost, willingness-to-pay and VAT/demand evidence is from Tanzania (AGRA 2019), while the causal loss-reduction is from Uganda (Omotilewa et al. 2018 RCT, re-analyzed in JSSF 2026), so this is a cross-country synthesis; the JSSF 2026 paper is a single-author secondary IV re-analysis rather than the primary field study; and neither source reports a standalone measured effect of point-of-sale micro-credit at scale. The Omotilewa et al. 2018 AJAE paper (the primary RCT) was not directly retrieved and is cited here as referenced in source [1].
  • Field validation for PICS-class hermetic bags exists only for dry-season pest storage of dry grain: a 4-cycle on-farm trial in the Mexican highlands (Odjo & Verhulst 2024, Field Crops Research) showed hermetic bags from three manufacturers kept moisture below 13%, insect damage below 5% and weight loss below 2% even after insect perforation and tape repair, over three-month cycles; a Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems 2022 analysis based on field-trial data quantified insect-loss reduction and GHG benefits for dry maize storage. Neither covers grain at 18-24% m.c.
  • The 2023 companion study (Yewle et al., Foods 12(4):891, same group) is a 21-day jar experiment giving the same qualitative result (18% m.c. ok ~14 days, 21% ok ~7 days, 24% fails) and explicitly calls for farm-level research 'on farms and along the grain value chain'; together the two lab studies bound the proposed safe operating window but provide no field or farm-scale evidence.
  • The burden scales to tonne-level: a 2025 CGIAR AICCRA brief (Zambia) states 'hand shelling can take about 14 labour-days to shell a ton of maize, the machine can do that in less than an hour', positioning the mechanised sheller as a low-cost labour-saving option for smallholders.
  • A low-cost mechanical shelling aid is established and cheap: a hand-operated maize sheller fabricated in Nigeria cost about $61 (NGN 27,100 at 1 USD = 447.52 NGN) and delivered 12-60 kg/h at 40-120 rpm, shelling efficiency 94-100%, kernel damage 0.12-0.95% (Okusanya & Agbongiaban 2023).
