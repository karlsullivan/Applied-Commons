This project is parked: Applied Commons has catalogued it but has not written a build pack for it.

Maslow4 is a low-cost CNC router that cuts full 1220 x 2440 mm (4 x 8 ft) sheets: a hand-held router body is held and moved by four belts anchored around the sheet. Firmware and control software are GPL-3.0 and the CAD files CC BY-SA, with some files under licences inherited from earlier projects. It brings sheet-goods fabrication, such as furniture and WikiHouse-style building parts, within reach of a household or community workshop.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • What the 'tolerance' actually is: WikiHouse bakes in a 0.25 mm offset on internal slots/holes and a 0.25 mm inset on outer profiles, giving 0.5 mm total joint clearance (an 18 mm ply slot becomes 18.5 mm). The guide states the primary driver is sheet-thickness variation - 18 mm ply actually arrives 17.1-18.1 mm thick (a 1.0 mm spread) - so the 0.5 mm clearance chiefly absorbs material variance rather than demanding sub-0.5 mm CNC positioning.
  • Authoritative production answer: the WikiHouse Manufacturing Guide states a Maslow is 'sufficient, and an affordable starting point' for prototyping, but 'for production purposes (generally projects needing more than 50 sheets cut) a higher performance, gantry-based CNC router is necessary.' So the design source itself does not expect a Maslow to hold the 0.5 mm join tolerance across repeated production; the community concurs, calling ±0.5 mm 'pretty close to the max for Maslow 4'.
  • The primary consumable is the belt: 4 steel-reinforced toothed TPU belts, 14.5 ft (4420 mm) long, ~58 g each, one per axis, wound on 4 polycarbonate spools gripped by 8 belt-end halves. Maslow sells a belt+spool replacement pair for ~$5.
  • No calendar or cut-hours service interval is published anywhere official. The only recurring instruction in the 4/4.1 Wisdom Manual is to 'regularly check your machine for evidence of wear or damage'; everything else is a symptom trigger, not a timetable.
  • The Maslow4 has NO mechanical endstops/limit switches. Homing is done by 4 AS5600 magnetic belt encoders (one per arm, on the encoder boards) plus belt-current feedback; the 'endstop' parts that wear are the 4 encoder boards and 4 encoder magnets.
  • Sled/linear bearings: 20 (4.0 kit) or 30 (4.1 kit) sealed 148ZZ ball bearings (8 mm bore) run the rollers and idlers; 4 linear bearings ride 2 x 10 mm hardened-steel rods for sled Z travel. These are standard catalog parts with trivial local substitutes.
  • Maslow 4 is officially designed for wood/plastic/flat material (README: 'wood, plastic or other flat material', ~60mm/2.5in deep per README and 70mm/2.75in per FAQ, on a 4x8 ft sheet); metal is a community capability, not a factory spec.
  • Vendor frame-rigidity requirement: anchors must be solid and not move in any direction under up to ~40 lb (~178 N) of pull, the cutting surface must be 'fairly stiff and flat' (a flexing/curved board rides the sled but degrades accuracy), anchors should sit in/near the plane of the workpiece, and the four anchors must fit within a 5.5 m x 5.5 m square. Vertical configurations are explicitly called out as needing more care because belt-tension stress at the top of the sheet differs greatly from the bottom, so vertical frames need extra rigidity at the top anchors; the default 2x4/2x8 wood frame achieves rigidity by 'engineering standard practice / felt right' rather than a quantified deflection target.
