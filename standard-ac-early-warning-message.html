CAP is the open international format for public warnings, used by national alerting authorities and meteorological services. It is free to implement, so community alerts can be fed into and read alongside official ones; a custom record would not interoperate with any of them. Issuing alerts at the gateway, with a human check, keeps a faulty sensor from raising a public warning by itself.

Specification

Early-warning alerts use the OASIS Common Alerting Protocol 1.2 (ITU-T X.1303bis). One alert per event, with identifier, sender, sent (per ac-iso8601-timestamps), status, msgType and scope. Its info block gives category (Geo, Met, Safety and so on), event, urgency, severity, certainty, onset and expires, headline, description and instruction (the recommended action), with one info block per local language (language identifier as CAP 1.2 specifies, per RFC 3066; current RFC 5646 tags are compatible). Area is a WGS 84 polygon or circle, or a geocode. Hazards in use: flood, drought, glacial lake outburst, storm, heat, cold, landslide, wildfire. A sensor node whose threshold or rate-of-rise logic trips sends its reading with a trip flag to its gateway; the gateway, not the node, issues the CAP alert, after a human confirms it unless the community has agreed automatic issue for that hazard. Delivery: CAP 1.2 XML to the warning platform; CAP defines XML and ASN.1 encodings only, so any JSON copy is a convenience and XML is the record. SMS, sirens, radio and the LoRa mesh carry the headline and instruction derived from the CAP message: an SMS holds up to 160 GSM-7 characters, or 70 UCS-2 characters for scripts outside GSM-7. The gateway also publishes the alert on the local MQTT broker under ac/<gateway-id>/alert, retained until it expires. Official national warnings take precedence: community alerts are labelled as community alerts and never presented as coming from the national authority, and public cell broadcast (EU-Alert, ETSI TS 102 900, in Europe) is left to that authority.
