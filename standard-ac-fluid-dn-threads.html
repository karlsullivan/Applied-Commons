BSP threads and PE pipe for module fluid ports

BSP threads and PE pipe for module fluid ports
Codeac-fluid-dn-threads
Kindfluid
StatusApproved
ApprovedOctober 2026

BSP is the pipe thread sold in Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia and South America; NPT is mainly North American. Mixing them is the high-risk fault the verification found. Parallel G ports with a washer can be remade many times, which suits swappable modules; taper-and-tape joints are designed to be made once. PE pipe with compression fittings is the cheapest pressure pipe almost everywhere (Estonia and most of Africa included), needs no threading tools and survives freezing better than rigid pipe. The DN table fixes the sizing errors the verification found.

Specification

Threads: BSP (Whitworth 55 degree) is the Applied Commons default worldwide. Module ports that get connected and disconnected (pumps, filters, meters, valves, heaters) use parallel G threads to ISO 228-1, sealed by a flat washer or O-ring, never by thread tape: G 1/2 by default for water, G 3/4 for hose and garden fittings. Permanent threaded joints use ISO 7-1 (EN 10226) R/Rp/Rc threads, sealed with PTFE tape or a sealant approved for the medium (gas-rated for biogas, drinking-water approved for potable water). NPT (ASME B1.20.1) only as a North American regional variant, with a labelled NPT-to-BSP adapter at every junction. NPT and BSP must never be mated directly: 55 vs 60 degrees with the same 14 TPI at 1/2 and 3/4 inch, so they look alike but leak. Pipe: polyethylene PE100 or PE80 to ISO 4427 / EN 12201, sized by outside diameter (20, 25, 32 mm), PN 10 minimum for pressurised water and PN 16 for pump delivery, joined with compression fittings to ISO 14236 with BSP threaded ends; steel pipe where needed to EN 10255. Sizes: DN is a size class, not inches: DN 8 = 1/4, DN 10 = 3/8, DN 15 = 1/2, DN 20 = 3/4, DN 25 = 1; 20 mm PE pairs with 1/2 inch fittings, 25 mm with 3/4, 32 mm with 1. Potable water: materials approved for drinking water under the local scheme ((EU) 2020/2184 Art. 11 in the EU), installed per EN 806 with backflow protection per EN 1717 wherever a module could contaminate the supply. Gas (biogas, LPG): pipework, joints and appliance connections per the local gas code (for example AS/NZS 5601.1, NFPA 54, national codes in Europe), pressure-tested and leak-checked before first use, by a qualified gas fitter where the code requires.
