RWSN-defined 'public domain' handpump specifications for the Afridev and the India Mark II (the world's most widely used handpump). Public-domain specs plus installation & maintenance manuals, an injection-moulding manual and quality-control guidelines; designed for local manufacturing and 'excellent potential for community-based maintenance', repairable by a village caretaker. Maturity: decades in use, distributed throughout Africa in the tens of thousands, public domain, RWSN-maintained. Directly meets the water requirement for open, asset-specific repair knowledge and locally buildable technician work.

Testing

CheckHowPass if
Pump delivers rated discharge at design lift (human power standardised at ~75 W input)Measure discharge into a calibrated container over a fixed time and a fixed stroke count at the site lift; compare against RWSN rated values (rated figures are given at 75 W of human input).Afridev: >=1.4 m3/h @10 m head, >=1.1 @15 m, >=0.9 @20 m, >=0.7 @30 m. India Mk II: >=1.8 m3/h @10 m, >=1.3 @15 m, >=1.0 @20 m, >=0.9 @25 m, >=0.8 @30 m (RWSN rated @75 W).
Sustained yield meets the community-service thresholdSustained pumping test; measure flow in L/min and confirm it holds without the point running dry.>=10 L/min yield (UPGro functionality yield threshold).
Pump primes to continuous water promptlyCount the number of strokes from a dry/first start to first continuous water flow.<=5 strokes to produce water (UPGro reliability / strokes-to-water threshold).
Volume per stroke is adequateCollect the volume delivered per single full stroke and repeat over several strokes.>=0.35 L/stroke (PLOS Water 2025 performance threshold; tested range 0.25-0.5 L/stroke).
Down-hole material is suitable to the site water (Mk II / any GI configuration)Measure site groundwater pH (and salinity/chloride) before installation; select riser/rod material accordingly.pH >= 6.5 required before installing India Mk II or GI components; where pH < 6.5 use stainless steel grade 304/316 or uPVC riser with stainless-steel couplers (Mk II 'should not be used in areas with aggressive water pH < 6.5').
Structural and installation stability of the assembled unitVisual and load check: pump head plumb, stand/cover anchored, platform cured and level; observe for movement under sustained pumping.No movement, settlement or loosening under sustained pumping; down-hole components jointed per the manual (PVC-U solvent-welded, rods straight and un-bent).
Drinking-water safety at the pointMicrobiological sample (E. coli / thermotolerable (faecal) coliform) per WHO sanitary inspection, plus visual/taste check for iron and red/rusty water.E. coli and thermotolerable coliform absent; water clear (no persistent red/rusty colour).

Failure modes

FailureEffectMitigation
Rapid corrosion of GI riser/rod in aggressive (low pH < 6.5) groundwaterRed, bitter-tasting water rejected by users; riser leaks then breaks; components rated 8-10 years fail within months to ~2 years; loss of safe supply.Use stainless steel grade 304/316 or uPVC with SS couplers in aggressive water; test pH before install; Uganda suspended GI (2016 letter, 2018 national directive).
Bent pumprodsPerforated riser pipe, loss of priming and discharge.Store rods flat and straight, free of bending; inspect for bends before/after installation (manual: 'bent rods are mostly the cause of perforated Riser pipes').
UV/heat degradation of uPVC riser and rubber seals (O-rings, cup/U seals)uPVC becomes brittle and cracks; rubber seals distort and fail; loss of priming and leaks.Store components shaded, cool, not crushed or in chemical/oil contact; stack riser pipes correctly; procure solvent cement fresh.
Inadequate galvanising or wrong stainless grade (e.g. grade 202)Rapid corrosion even in mildly aggressive or non-aggressive water.Specify grade 304/316, require supplier certification and clear labelling; inspect galvanising quality.
Galvanic corrosion from dissimilar metals in the submerged assemblyAccelerated corrosion at joints (witnessed 'mixed' GI + stainless installs).Use a compatible material combination for submerged parts; avoid GI + stainless contact in solution.
Insufficient aquifer yield / low transmissivity / droughtPump runs dry; water point fails independent of the pump (a hydrogeological, not mechanical, failure).Hydrogeological (VES) survey before drilling/install, recovery test, and match lift to static water level and recharge.
Wearing-parts wear (foot valve, plunger/cup seals, rising-main centralisers)Loss of prime, reduced discharge, leaks.Village-level scheduled replacement per the manual's replacement intervals; Afridev's open-top cylinder allows piston removal without dismantling the rising main and a retractable foot valve with fishing tool.
Poor PVC-U jointing / stale solvent cementJoint leaks and riser separation.Correct solvent-cement technique, buy cement shortly before use, observe cure; joint per the manual.

Known gaps

What the original design does not document:

  • No per-component unit cost with supplier/region/date is published in any open source retrieved; Afridev Annex II (List of Spare Parts, with prices) and India Mark II Annex 2 / Annex 4 (fast-moving and full spare-parts lists with drawing numbers) are image-only tables in the manuals and were not text-extractable, so their part numbers and prices could not be transcribed.
  • Exact riser-pipe / pumprod (Afridev) and riser-pipe / pumprod (Mark II) counts depend on borehole depth and static/dynamic water level, which the manuals leave to site survey; a fixed total per depth is therefore not stated in the sources.
  • Afridev drawing numbers (B2003, B2050/B2055, A2099/A2119, A2070/A2257/A2296, A2206/A2209/A2110/A5889) are quoted from the options table, but the underlying full technical drawings are a separate RWSN 'Handpump Specification' set not retrieved here.
  • Platform material quantities are the manual's borehole-platform basis; dug-well platform quantities are 'calculated per well' and not fixed.
  • The engineeringforchange.org mirror of the Mk II manual was Cloudflare-gated (curl returned a challenge page); the Mk II manual was instead retrieved from the RWSN Library direct file URL, so some Mk II part numbers (A2515, A2443, A2470, A2478, C2476, C2477, B2420, C1005/C1137) come from the RWSN-published edition text.
  • Ball-bearing type/size for the Mark II handle and exact cup-seal dimensions are not stated in the extracted text (shown in manual diagrams).
  • The pump bodies themselves (Afridev and India Mark II) have no open CAD/STEP/SolidWorks 3D model or open mechanical drawings in any git repository - their only published design sources are the RWSN PDF specifications (dimensioned specs + part numbers) and the Afridev rubber-component mould drawings; no editable 3D geometry is openly available.
  • No git repository (GitHub) hosts the RWSN Afridev / India Mark II design documents - they are static PDFs served from rural-water-supply.net, so there is no cloneable repo or pinned commit for the pump design itself.
  • The RWSN public-domain status is stated in prose ('public domain pump defined by RWSN specifications') but no SPDX licence id or formal open-licence deed is published for the specifications or manuals.
  • Afridev sensor gen-1 repo (charitywater/afridev-sensor) has no open electrical schematic PDF and no pick/place; only Altium gerbers, STEP enclosure and ME PDF drawings - the schematic is not included.
  • India Mark II sensor repo (charitywater/india-mark-ii-sensor) has no CAD/3D enclosure model and no PCB/gerber fab files (only electrical PDFs + STM32/FreeRTOS/AWS firmware); the mechanical enclosure and board fab data are not published.
  • No BOM (bill of materials) as a standalone spreadsheet/CSV is published for the Afridev or India Mark II pump bodies - part lists are embedded within the RWSN specification PDFs.
  • charitywater/afridev2-sensor and india-mark-ii-sensor have no repository-level licence (top-level) - only the v2 sensor firmware subtree is Apache-2.0, so the CAD/gerber hardware files' re-use rights are unspecified.
  • These three Charity Water repositories are telemetry/monitoring sensors that mount ON the pumps, not the pumps themselves; no open build files exist for the pump's castings, pump rod, cylinder, or platform.
  • No single open, consolidated acceptance-test procedure with numeric pass/fail targets for a finished, field-assembled unit; the open manuals give installation/repair sequences and rated discharge but no formal acceptance checklist.
  • Rated discharge is given only 'approximate at 75 W input'; the stroke cadence/effort that realises 75 W is not specified, so discharge targets need a standardised stroke rate to be reproducible.
  • No field-verification method for confirming the stainless-steel grade at installation (Stop the Rot: 'no procedures or simple guidance available to easily determine the stainless steel grade').
  • Corrosion-resistance acceptance is only a pH threshold (<6.5); no packaged Langelier-index / corrosion-risk-mapping acceptance step for the unit.
  • Depth limitations of alternative materials (stainless ~45 m; uPVC+SS 30-39 m; uPVC+uPVC ~9 m) are recognised from field experience but 'have not been documented, nor set out in any guidelines'.
  • No unit-level microbiological sampling procedure; drinking-water safety is delegated to WHO source sanitary inspection rather than a pump-unit test.
  • The only located published handpump performance-test standard (PNS-PAES-154:2010) is host-gated (Scribd) and agriculture-oriented; ISO 14686:2003 is a well pumping (hydrogeology) test, not a pump-unit acceptance test. No free full text of a dedicated Afridev/Mk II acceptance standard was retrieved.

Safety

Human-powered suction handpump; no electrical/battery/gas/radio in the build pack. Real hazards are water/biological (HIGH), chemical (MEDIUM), structural (MEDIUM), mechanical (LOW), heat-fire (LOW) and pressure (LOW). All have established, low-cost controls; most are already in the build pack's safety/test sections. Safe to build/install/use with the controls listed. The only step that can require a competent/qualified person is dug-well open-excavation work and its reinforced cover (community borehole platform does not).

HazardSeverityControls
Microbial contamination of the potable supply (E. coli / thermotolerant (faecal) coliform), incl. from unhygienic source, storage, spout or handling; and from the well before disinfection.highDisinfect the well shortly before installation: 300 g bleaching powder in 15 L (borehole) or 150-200 g/m3 of water stored (dug well), per the Afridev manual; Test the finished supply: E. coli / thermotolerant coliform must not be detectable in any 100 mL sample before the point is released to the community (WHO GDWQ4 Table 7.10); Sanitary siting and design: platform sealed against surface runoff, spout draining clear of contamination, adequate separation from latrines/septic, assessed for flooding and drought exposure; Community hygiene training on collection and safe storage (narrow-opening / spigot container)
Heavy-metal leaching from water-contact parts into drinking water: lead (from non-lead-free uPVC/brass/solder) and metals released by rapid corrosion of galvanised steel (GI) or wrong-grade stainless (e.g. 202) in aggressive (low pH) groundwater, before any visible rust.highSpecify lead-free uPVC and stainless steel grade 304/316 for down-hole parts; require supplier certification and clear labelling; inspect galvanising quality at delivery; Measure site groundwater pH before installation; do NOT use India Mk II / GI (mild-steel) components where pH < 6.5 (manual: MS rods 'not recommended when PH value is 6.5'); use SS 304/316 or uPVC+SS couplers instead; Avoid galvanic corrosion: use compatible materials in the submerged assembly, do not mix GI and stainless in solution without appropriate couplings; Verify water is clear (no persistent red/rusty colour) and test for lead where corrosion is suspected

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • The ~175,000 figure is specifically the count of non-functional HANDPUMP water points in sub-Saharan Africa in 2015 (about one in four of the handpump base), from Foster, Furey, Banks & Willetts (2019), International Journal of Water Resources Development. It is not the count of all WASH water points, and the implied SSA handpump base is roughly 700,000 (175k / 0.25).
  • The India Mark II manual (and the WFPIT repair manual) contains an explicit troubleshooting table ('General Problems ... with Rectification') that enumerates the known, manual-listed faults and their causes: handle operates without pumping water (causes: dry well / no water at source, lost priming, worn cylinder cup washer, skidding connecting rod, broken pump rod, riser-pipe leakage, cylinder-bottom gasket leak, cracked cylinder, choked suction pipe); low water delivery (damaged plunger leather, poor well recuperation, valve leak); too many strokes to deliver water (priming misalignment, damaged cylinder seals); handle rises automatically after down-stroke (chock in riser/cylinder, suction pipe too short, water level below cylinder); excessive noise (loose head-assembly parts, wrong-size connecting rods, loose rods, mis-levelled top flange, loosened ground fixing, dried lubricant).
  • RWSN India Mark II specification (the pump in the project name): the pump 'requires special skills for installation as well as for the maintenance; it is not considered to be a VLOM pump'; 'To service the INDIA Mark II Pump skills and tools are needed which exceeds the ability of a village-level caretaker. However trained area mechanics can successfully maintain the pump'; installation needs 'well trained area mechanics or a mobile team with lifting tackle and comprehensive tool kit'. Interpretation: RWSN's own design position is that village-level repair knowledge is NOT expected for Mark II - the repair interface is the trained area mechanic tier, so 'missing repair knowledge' at village level is a designed state, not the documented failure mode. The documented failure interface is mechanic availability/reach plus parts availability.
  • Harvey 2011 (RWSN Field Note 2011-1): 'One of the primary reasons for low levels of sustainability is that supply chains do not deliver spare parts close to customers at an affordable cost. Typically, spares are not readily available.' Margins on spare parts are considerably lower than on pumps, so there are few incentives for national or local private businesses to stock and sell spare parts; recommends pumps and spares follow the same distribution path and linking product supply with technical services (installation, repair, rehabilitation) to create a chain of private-sector incentives.
  • AO/Concern 2015 supply-chain study (DRC): WASH DRC stakeholders 'ranked access to spare parts as the most important hand pump sustainability challenge'; 'a dearth of trained mechanics/technicians' confirmed as an important challenge; a UNICEF/A-Aqua 2014 study found no spare-parts supply chain existed in 3 of 5 Eastern DRC regions (the other 2 served only by cross-border suppliers from Uganda); WMCs had no knowledge of where to obtain spare parts apart from NGO partners, and initial NGO spare-part stocks were typically depleted within two years; the study identifies an 'NGO-induced demand-side market failure' (direct INGO procurement from India suppresses local stocking).
  • Foster et al. 2018 (UPGro research brief, Kwale County Kenya; 337 matched Afridev boreholes, 30-year retrospective survival analysis; 64% functional at avg age 24 years): distance to spare parts was a significant failure risk factor - 'A 1km increase in distance to spare part suppliers was associated with 2% increase in risk of failure and a 2% reduction in lifespan' (alongside salinity +2% per 100 uS/cm, water level +3% per m, unconsolidated sand aquifer +70-108%). This is the best public quantification of 'spare part unavailable within reach' as a distinct, measured driver of pumps staying broken.
  • Afridev is a public-domain, corrosion-resistant (PVC-U riser, brass-lined cylinder), village-level maintainable (VLOM) derivative-lineage deep-well pump 'distributed throughout Africa, many ten thousands'; the India Mark II is NOT corrosion-resistant (galvanised-iron riser and rods, cast-iron/brass cylinder) and is NOT a VLOM pump - it needs a trained area mechanic. RWSN's own standardisation case notes JICA deliberately chose Afridev over India Mk II in Zambia for this reason, and newer pumps (e.g. BluePump) are marketed as replacements for 'an Afridev or India Mark II/III', corroborating that these two dominate the SSA fleet.
  • The manuals themselves classify 'no water at the source / well is dry' as a fault, but its listed remedy is re-boring to depth or lowering the cylinder / adding riser pipe - i.e. NOT a pump-mechanical repair. This means a real, manual-listed class of 'non-functioning' water points is unfixable by pump knowledge at all.
