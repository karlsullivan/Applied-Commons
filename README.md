# Applied Commons

**Engineering practical solutions for the common good.**

Applied Commons is a for-purpose engineering initiative focused on developing practical, reproducible solutions to environmental, humanitarian, and public-interest challenges.

The emphasis is on **building, testing, measuring, and publishing**.

We are interested in problems where useful technologies may be overlooked because they are difficult to commercialise, span multiple disciplines, or primarily create public benefit.

## Principles

Applied Commons is built around a few simple principles:

* Build practical systems, not just concepts.
* Publish designs, methods, data, and failures where possible.
* Prefer reproducible engineering over advocacy or speculation.
* Measure real outcomes rather than activity.
* Design for accessibility and replication.
* Encourage collaboration across engineering, science, research, and field practice.
* Keep public-benefit work available for non-commercial use.
* Treat commercialisation as a means to scale impact where appropriate, not as the primary objective.

Progress is measured in **experiments, designs, datasets, deployments, and verified outcomes — not meetings, memberships, funding, or publicity.**

## How We Work

Projects should move toward tangible outputs such as:

* experimental hardware
* software
* datasets
* test results
* reproducible designs
* engineering analysis
* documented negative results
* field evidence

Where possible, experiments should document:

1. the problem or hypothesis
2. the proposed approach
3. the design and bill of materials
4. the test method
5. raw or processed data
6. results
7. limitations and failures
8. next steps

A failed experiment that is properly documented is still a useful contribution.

## Contributing

Applied Commons welcomes contributions from engineers, researchers, scientists, makers, field practitioners, students, and anyone able to contribute useful technical work.

Contributions can include:

* research and prior-art discovery
* engineering design
* software
* electronics and instrumentation
* modelling and analysis
* experimental testing
* documentation
* replication of previous experiments
* review by subject-matter experts

See `CONTRIBUTING.md` for contribution guidelines.

## Safety

Some Applied Commons projects may involve hazardous equipment, chemicals, electrical systems, pressure, heat, field environments, or safety-critical applications.

Published work should be treated as experimental unless explicitly demonstrated otherwise.

See `SAFETY.md` for more information.

## Status

Applied Commons is at an early stage.

The initial focus is on establishing a lightweight framework for practical, openly developed engineering work without creating unnecessary organisational overhead.

The first active technical work will be developed under the OpenCarbon project, with MineDetector maintained initially as a future research stream.
