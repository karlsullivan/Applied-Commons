Means of production

From Applied Commons, the open catalogue of ways to meet human needs
A planning and research library, not a blueprint. Staged from ac-tool-library v0.4 without changing Applied Commons' own records. Equipment entries are candidates to assess access to, not a list to buy or build. Process routes are conceptual: quantities, yields and capacities are unknown. Designs are located, not audited. Revenue is potential until evidenced. Nothing here shows that a process is safe, feasible or profitable.

Applied Commons aims to decentralise and reinvent the means of production. For a small community that means delivering useful services locally, sharing productive assets, and taking part in a regional network of specialist producers: not running every industrial process on one site.

Start with the eight local service systems below. Each lists the equipment worth assessing, grouped by scope, the enterprises it could support, the process routes behind them, and the research still needed.

How to read this library

  • Core candidates: Worth assessing access to locally: owning is one option; sharing, hiring and buying the service are others.
  • Optional enterprises: Only if a chosen enterprise needs them and its demand, labour and capital are credible.
  • Regional specialists: Better served by a nearby specialist shared by several communities than duplicated.
  • External supplies and research: Bought in, or long-term research: never a default community requirement.
  • Not yet classified: Scope not yet assessed: classify by actual demand and scale before relying on it.
  • All of means every part is needed; One of means any one alternative will do. Alternatives are kept: buying, sharing, restoring and building can each meet a need.
  • Unknown means not yet established. It is never zero and never a failure.
  • Evidence states follow Applied Commons' rules: an inference is labelled as one, and a located source is not a validated design.
  • Maslow needs remain the main way into the site: Needs. This library is a planning view beside them.

A practical route

  1. Choose a service boundary and map existing producers: A dated requirements and resource profile; missing facts recorded
  2. Close one repair-to-production loop: A repeatable part and paid or otherwise evidenced service outcome
  3. Link two productive enterprises: A traceable cross-workshop transaction without double-counted value
  4. Prove a shared module: Independent fit, function and rated-duty evidence
  5. Reduce a critical dependency: A demonstrated reduction in disruption risk, not just a new catalogue entry
  6. Replicate the production network: Independent reproduction with evidenced service, access and economics
