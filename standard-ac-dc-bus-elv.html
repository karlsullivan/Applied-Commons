Off-grid homesteads, small farms and villages, and most of rural Africa, run on 12 or 24 V solar battery systems. Parts (solar charge controllers, DC pumps, lights, fridges, fuses, cable) are sold everywhere for solar, automotive and marine use, and extra-low voltage needs no electrician for module wiring. PoE and USB-C both need a source on site; this bus is that source when there is no mains. The maintainer flagged the missing DC bus standard on 2026-10-07.

Specification

Nominal voltages: 12, 24 or 48 V DC from a battery (lead-acid or LiFePO4) charged by solar or mains, as in solar home systems and small off-grid sites (IEC TS 62257 series). One bus voltage per site: 12 V up to about 300 W of load, 24 V up to about 1.5 kW, 48 V above that. Module inputs accept the whole battery range of their nominal voltage: 10.5-15 V for 12 V, 21-30 V for 24 V, 42-60 V for 48 V. Charge settings keep a 48 V bus at or below 60 V at all times; no absorb, float or equalise setting may exceed it. Every module input has reverse-polarity protection and a TVS clamp. Protection: a fuse or DC-rated circuit breaker at the battery terminal, and one on every branch, each sized to its cable; the battery fuse is rated to interrupt the battery's short-circuit current. Breakers must be DC-rated, because AC breakers do not reliably interrupt DC arcs. Cable sized for at most 3 % voltage drop at full load; polarity marked on every conductor and connector. Connectors: XT60 for module branches up to 20 A, with the female (shrouded) side on the supply so live contacts cannot be touched; ring lugs on fused bus bars above that. Low-draw modules take 5 V USB-C (ac-dc-usb-c) from a DC-DC converter on the bus. PoE nodes use a PoE switch or injector with a 12/24/48 V input (ac-poe-power-data) whose ports are limited power sources. Batteries: stationary lithium packs to IEC 62619, small packs to IEC 62133-2, each with a battery management system; installed per the local code, ventilated for lead-acid. Power conversion equipment (chargers, charge controllers, DC-DC converters, PoE injectors) meets IEC 62109-1; lead-acid charge controllers also meet IEC 62509 for performance. Safety basis: the bus is an extra-low-voltage installation under IEC 60364-4-41 (SELV or PELV, up to 120 V DC); each module and piece of equipment on it is ES1 under IEC 62368-1 (up to 60 V DC). The hazard is the battery's short-circuit current (fire), which the battery fuse controls.
