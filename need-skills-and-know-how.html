The need

184M people in rural sub-Saharan Africa depend on handpumps and 400M+ people in India alone (Foster et al. 2019). 2,884 health facilities across 39 low- and middle-income countries (Global Fund/Lancet Global Health 2025 facility survey). 76M energy-sector workers globally, of whom applied-technical roles are over half the workforce (IEA WEE 2025). The entire global population's 62 billion kg (7.8 kg per capita) of 2022 e-waste (ITU/UNITAR 2024). In short: rural water users in SSA/South Asia, LMIC patients and health facilities, energy/utility workers, and electronics consumers in low-income regions (Africa <1% formal e-waste recycling).

Severe and persistent in every sector. Water: ~1 in 4 handpumps non-functional at any time in sub-Saharan Africa (~175,000 inoperative water points in 2015); earlier RWSN estimates 36% non-functional, country range 10-65%; described as a 'persistent and widespread' sustainability problem. Health: 40-70% of medical devices/equipment in resource-poor settings broken, unused or unfit for purpose; only 58.6% of 2,884 surveyed LMIC facilities offered oxygen therapy, and among those, less than half had reliable oxygen over the preceding 3 months; oxygen gaps contribute to an estimated 9 million deaths per year in LMICs from conditions that could require medical oxygen - gaps 'documented for decades'. Electronics: only 22.3% of 62 billion kg e-waste formally collected/recycled in 2022 (Africa <1% vs Europe 42.8%); waste growth has outpaced formal recycling by ~5x since 2010. Energy: >60% of firms report labour shortages (a steady multi-year increase); applied-technical demand grew 16% vs 9% vocational graduations (2015-2022); 2 of every 3 new energy hires by 2035 will be needed just to replace retirees. All structural, not episodic.

What people do now

Water: capital is invested in building new water points while operations & maintenance are underfunded - a 'multibillion-dollar finance gap'; after construction the only levers are tariffs, cost cuts, or subsidies (RWSN, 213 government investment cases from 68 countries). Health: equipment is procured for high-income markets and 'installation, preventive and corrective maintenance services and user training programs are also traditionally absent in LMICs' (PMC systematic review). Energy: ~50% of firms are recruiting from neighbouring industries or building in-house training to fill technical gaps (IEA WEE 2025). Electronics: 77.7% of e-waste (100% - 22.3% formally recycled) is not formally collected/recycled, i.e. informal channels dominate, near-total in Africa (<1% formal). Cost signal: closing the energy skills gap would cost ~USD 2.6B/year worldwide - less than 0.1% of global public education spending (IEA).

Where existing solutions fall short

Existing solutions miss the actual failing assets. Open-build-guide programmes (Fab Lab Network's ~1,500 labs in 90+ countries; the Mozilla-supported Open Hardware Makers Curriculum) serve maker/hobbyist communities with no standard curriculum tied to the specific essential infrastructure that is breaking - handpumps, oxygen systems, medical devices, rural energy assets. EU right-to-repair (Directive 2024/1799, with a European Repair Platform) forces manufacturers to supply repair information and spare parts, but only for goods in the EU market, while the assets failing in LMICs are often imported, donated, or legacy equipment with no repair information at all. In health, the root causes include procurement-maintenance mismatch - devices bought for rich-country conditions with maintenance and training 'traditionally absent' - so know-how does not travel with the hardware. In energy, ~50% of firms resort to poaching from other industries or ad-hoc in-house training because formal vocational graduation (+9% 2015-2022) trails demand (+16%); there is no open, reusable, regionally transferable technician curriculum at the required scale. And for water, the 13 documented government financing tactics address the money side of the O&M gap but not the skills side - there is no standardized, open water-maintenance technician programme covering the sub-Saharan pump base.

What a solution must do

  • Water: open, asset-specific repair and maintenance knowledge (schematics, part numbers, step-by-step repair guides) for the dominant rural handpump models, enabling a locally trained technician to restore a broken pump in the field - target: raise rural water-point functionality from ~75% to >=90% in sub-Saharan Africa (vs ~25% non-functionality today; Foster et al. 2019)
  • Water: locally buildable technician training programmes that place and retain at least one trained handpump technician per service area, bundled with equipment deployment because O&M funding is structurally thin (RWSN 2026)
  • Health: open installation, preventive/corrective maintenance and user-training packages shipped with every medical device sold or donated to LMICs - target: reduce the 40-70% share of devices broken, unused or unfit for purpose (PMC systematic review)
  • Health: oxygen-system competence - maintenance schedules and oxygen-concentration control at all facility levels - targeting reliable oxygen availability (no 3-month outage) in a majority of LMIC facilities (currently <50%; Global Fund/Lancet Global Health 2025)
  • Energy: an open, regionally scalable applied-technical training pipeline that grows trained-technician output by ~40% by 2030 (IEA estimate, ~USD 2.6B/year globally, <0.1% of world education spending) so electrification and maintenance are not bottlenecked by skills
  • Electronics: free, open repair guides plus spare-parts and repair-service information for the top household electronics, meeting or exceeding the standard now imposed on EU manufacturers by Directive (EU) 2024/1799 (repair within a reasonable time at a reasonable price, from 31 July 2026)

Constraints

  • Applied-technical roles are over half the energy workforce yet the hardest to fill; 2 of every 3 new energy hires by 2035 must simply replace retiring workers (IEA WEE 2025)
  • Ageing workforce: in advanced economies 2.4 workers approach retirement per worker under 25; in nuclear 1.7 and in grid roles 1.4 per entrant (IEA WEE 2025)
  • Women make up less than 5% of technical and vocational energy workers - any programme that does not recruit broadly leaves most of the labour pool unused (IEA WEE 2025)
  • Operational (O&M) costs are not covered by repayable finance that funds construction; governments are limited to tariff increases, cost cuts, and subsidies - training spend must fit inside this envelope (RWSN 2026)
  • Much equipment is designed and marketed for high-income settings; manufacturers are not attuned to harsh LMIC deployment conditions (high heat, fluctuating electricity, no clean water), so generic guides without local adaptation fail (PMC systematic review)
  • AI/productivity tools do not reduce demand for manual operations, maintenance and construction roles - the skills must be human (IEA WEE 2025)
  • Open maker networks (Fab Labs: ~1,500 labs in 90+ countries) are community-driven and concentrated, not scaled to rural low-income coverage (Fab Foundation)
