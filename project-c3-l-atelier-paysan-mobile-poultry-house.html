This project is parked: Applied Commons has catalogued it but has not written a build pack for it.

A self-build timber poultry house with plans from L'Atelier Paysan, the French cooperative of farm-built tools. It is a larger structure than a small chicken tractor; the source leaves out interior nests and perches and describes mobility as still prototyped. Production library design audit AUD02: assess it against flock size and the equipment available to move it, and specify nests, predator protection and access.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • The named 42 m2 'Poulailler mobile' mobility system is still in prototyping per L'Atelier Paysan's own primary page (one coop built to the plans); no dedicated R&D forum page documents it as finalized or costed
  • The official 42 m2 (6 m x 7 m) wood-frame mobile coop has floor area 42 m2, is sized for 250 laying hens per the organic spec, has 4 hatches + 2 openings + 2 large doors, deliberately NO floor (manure left in field), needs 'no less than 10 days' of work for one experienced person, and uses sawn sections bastaing 175x50, chevrons 75x50, pannes 220x45 (the plans' nomenclature; the official webpage prints a 220*145 typo for the purlins), voliges 225x25, with a 100 mm insulated steel (bac acier) roof on 5% pitch, anti-condensation film, and wood cladding (bardage en clain).
  • The on-rails mobility variant was abandoned ('tue dans l'oeuf'); stated reasons: cost of the rail, weeding of the rail (brushcutter), and terrain planarity; only an Arduino macquette (solar, autonomous move, light-based open/close) was made, with no cost figures published
  • L'Atelier Paysan itself names the structure's main limitation as 'the cost of the structure, which requires a lot of sawn wood', and the plans state that the design is deliberately scalable - 'a mobile coop of smaller surface is entirely imaginable from these plans, by simply transposing the dimensions' (e.g. 36 m2 can suffice for broilers). So the 7,230 EUR figure is specific to the full 42 m2 / 250-hen version, not to the concept.
  • Laying-hen thermoneutral/comfort zone is ~18–22 °C (breeder technical update states comfort 18–25 °C; peer-reviewed study reports TNR 19–22 °C, body temp 40.6–42.4 °C).
  • A field-validated on-axle method exists — lift the coop and slide in a single steerable axle (two non-steerable axles named a weakness) — documented in a forum thread explicitly titled 'Poulailler mobile sur essieux : teste & valide !' on a 35 m2 organic broiler coop
  • Cost of that validated 35 m2 coop is stated as 4,000 EUR raw materials (no salvage); the mobility hardware (single axle + wheels/steering, recovered from a scrapyard) is quoted as 'not more than 100 EUR'. This is the whole-coop figure, not a mobility-only line item, and it is a 35 m2 design not the 42 m2 house
  • The PDG 250 (the org's 'most advanced' of its two mobile-coop approaches) uses a slide-in axle + hitch trailer; a dedicated 'Remorque PDG' forum thread records field-tested hitching: a single 80x10 mm perforated flat bar as the tractor-to-trailer link (one farm), or a standard ball with a 'tete de lapin' ball-head adapter fitted via a round tube and two bolt holes
