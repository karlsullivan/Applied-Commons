Tanks, filters, pipes, cold stores and food-processing modules all put materials against food or water, and a bad choice (lead, unknown recycled plastic, printed parts) quietly harms the people the module is meant to help. The EU and NSF schemes are the established lists; naming the basis in the build pack makes it checkable.

Specification

Parts that touch food or drinking water are made of materials approved for that contact. In the EU: food contact under Regulation (EC) 1935/2004, plastics under Regulation (EU) 10/2011 with a declaration of compliance from the supplier, made under good manufacturing practice (Regulation (EC) 2023/2006); drinking-water contact under Directive (EU) 2020/2184 Article 11 and its implementing acts (positive lists and acceptance tests, applying from 31 December 2026; national approval schemes until then). In North America: NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 for drinking water and NSF/ANSI 51 for food equipment. Elsewhere, the national scheme, or failing one, materials listed under the EU or NSF schemes. Preferred materials: food-grade stainless steel (304 or 316); HDPE and PP from suppliers that declare food or drinking-water compliance; glass; lead-free brass. Not allowed in contact: lead, lead solder or leaded brass, cadmium, recycled plastics of unknown origin, galvanised steel with acidic foods, and ordinary 3D-printing filament or printed parts (layer gaps harbour bacteria and filaments are rarely certified) unless the material and finish are certified for that use. Gaskets, sealants and lubricants count as contact materials. The build pack names each contact material and its compliance basis.
