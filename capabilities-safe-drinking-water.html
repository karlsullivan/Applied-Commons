Reliable access to safe drinking water

From Applied Commons, the open catalogue of ways to meet human needs
Reliable access to safe drinking water
NeedWater
Capabilities5
Designs14

Every household has enough safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene, close to home, all year round, including dry seasons and freezing winters.

Thresholds

LevelWhat it means
MinimumEmergency minimum (Sphere 2018): at least 15 l per person per day for drinking and domestic hygiene; water point under 500 m from any household; queuing under 30 minutes; at point of delivery under 10 CFU/100 ml (unchlorinated) or 0.2-0.5 mg/l free residual chlorine (chlorinated); turbidity under 5 NTU; clean, covered storage.[1][2][3]
BaselineE. coli (or thermotolerant coliforms) not detectable in any 100 ml sample at the point of use (WHO GDWQ Table 7.10), from a protected source, stored safely, reliably all year.[4]
ImprovedSafe water on or near the premises, continuously available, with less labour, energy and cost, and built and repaired locally.

Improvements sought

  • less collection time and labour
  • lower energy or fuel per litre treated
  • lower lifetime cost
  • greater local manufacturability and repair
  • year-round operation in freezing climates
  • residual protection against recontamination in storage

Current situation

Judged per setting. On 2026-10-21 the Applied Commons catalogue held no open implementation of any of the seven capabilities below (its Water projects are simulation software, sensors and irrigation controllers).

Capabilities

Safe water storage

Keeping treated water safe until use: covered, narrow-necked household containers, and tanks for households and communities.

DesignIndustry levelEvidence
Locally made clay storage pot with lid and tap
A fired clay pot with a fitted lid and a tap or narrow spout, made by local potters.		B1E3 (claimed)
Ferrocement storage tank
A cylindrical or pumpkin-shaped tank of cement mortar on wire mesh, for households and institutions.		B1-B2E4
Covered narrow-necked containers (10-20 l)
Two containers per household, one to collect and one to store, with a narrow neck or tap so hands do not enter the water.		B5E4

Water source development

Obtaining water from groundwater (dug or drilled wells), springs, rain or surface water, with the source protected from contamination.

DesignIndustry levelEvidence
Manually drilled borehole (auger, jetting, sludging or percussion)
Small-diameter wells drilled by hand with locally made tools, typically 15-40 m depending on technique and geology.		B1-B2E3
Roof rainwater harvesting into a ferrocement tank
Gutters and a first-flush diverter feeding a ferrocement tank.		B1E4
Protected spring (spring box)
A masonry or concrete box enclosing a spring eye, with an overflow, a cover and a fenced, drained catchment.		B1E4 (claimed)

Water distribution

Getting water from the source to households: carrying in containers, gravity-fed pipes and tapstands, pumped networks.

DesignIndustry levelEvidence
Carrying water from the water point
Households collect in containers from a water point within walking distance.		B0E5 (claimed)
Gravity-fed piped supply to tapstands
A protected source above the community feeding a storage tank and buried pipes to public tapstands.		B2-B5E4

Water lifting (pumping)

Raising water from a well, borehole or tank to where it is used: buckets and windlasses, hand pumps, rope pumps, motorised pumps.

DesignIndustry levelEvidence
Afridev public-domain hand pump

Rope and washer pump (rope pump)
An endless rope with washers drawn up a rising pipe by a hand-cranked wheel; made in small local workshops.		B1-B2E3
