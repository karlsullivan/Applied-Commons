Open Source Ecology's Universal Rotor: a common rotary drive module meant to serve several Global Village Construction Set machines. Production library design audit AUD07 (shared rotary drive): retrieve the drawings and interfaces, choose two real applications, and test whether one module can serve both.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • The $250 is a stated production-cost GOAL, not a realized cost; verbatim from the requirements page the $250 covers materials + hydraulic motor for a single rotor unit, with a separate $500 prototype cost goal.
  • No measured continuous-duty data exist for the Universal Rotor at the 15,000 in-lb spec. OSE documents no sustained (continuous) torque, sustained speed/RPM, or thermal (temperature) limit. The product page is still a template (spec fields read 'Describe features of Module 1' / 'Key spec data point'), the R&D page states it 'is currently in the prototyping phase', the development table lists every stage (Design, Build, Lifecycle) at 0%, and the 'Universal Rotor Data Collection' page does not exist (404).
  • There is NO public evidence that a 3" keyed shaft + double-chain coupler survives a full season of hammermill/pelletizer operation without keyway or coupling wear, because the end machines are unbuilt: 'The Hammermill is currently in the research phase of development' (Chipper Hammermill, actuation by Hydraulic Motor, power by Power Cube) and 'The Pelletizer is currently in the research phase of development.' No season-of-operation, keyway-wear, or coupling-wear data for these applications exists in the sources.
  • The only realized cost data is a 2012 parts order totaling $2,738.70 in which four 45.6-cu-in wheel motors alone were $1,639.80 (~$410 each); the wheel-motor component alone therefore already exceeds the $250 all-in materials-plus-motor goal.
  • 15,000 in-lb is the nominal design/spec torque; the '30,000 lb' half of the question's spec appears in no OSE source. 15,000 in-lb is the torque rating of the 15,000 Inch Pound Motor, which 'Universal Rotor 2' defines as the rotor's motor ('this is the 15,000 in lb motor version, at Universal Rotor'). The only other manufacturer-sourced load figure is the motor's radial capacity, 'up to 11,000 [lb] of radial load per the specs provided by the manufacturer'; no axial, torsional, or '30,000 lb' spec is published.
  • The double-chain coupler is a 2012 design, not a proven field component: 'Prototype #3 - double chain sprocket coupler. Advantage: larger radius to withstand 15,000 inch pounds of torque' (Modular Wheel Units, Jan 27 2012). It was conceived after the Oct 2011 key shear to increase the shear radius. No test or field data document how it performs under sustained load.
  • No realized lifetime-cost figure and no maintenance interval (bearing replacement, keyway re-milling, seal, or axial-thrust wear) is published anywhere in OSE's public wiki or the 2012 ESW development record; the design is still in prototyping and the improved-rotor bill of materials was an unmet deliverable.
  • The only durability evidence is qualitative field-failure reporting, not a quantified wear interval: 3/8-inch outward shaft slippage observed after 20 hours of skid-steer axial testing (retention was only two double-split collars; the fix was adding two more), a sheared motor-shaft key, and a catastrophic Oct 2011 wheel-coupler failure when used with tracks.
