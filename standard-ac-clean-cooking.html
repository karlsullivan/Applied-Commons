Smoke from cooking on open fires and simple stoves is one of the largest health risks among the needs Applied Commons covers. ISO 19867 is the international test and rating scheme, and the WHO emission rates are the health-based targets. A CO alarm costs little and prevents the most common fatal accident.

Specification

Cooking stove modules are tested with the harmonised laboratory protocol of ISO 19867-1 and rated on the ISO/TR 19867-3 voluntary performance tiers (0 to 5) for each of its five parameters: thermal efficiency, PM2.5 emissions, CO emissions, safety and durability. Stoves used indoors meet the emission rates of the WHO indoor air quality guidelines for household fuel combustion (2014): PM2.5 at most 0.23 mg/min unvented or 0.80 mg/min vented, and CO at most 0.16 g/min unvented or 0.59 g/min vented. A design that cannot meet the unvented rates is installed with a chimney or hood and meets the vented rates. Applied Commons stoves aim for the highest tiers they can reach and publish all five results. Results come from a laboratory running ISO 19867-1, or from a field approximation clearly labelled as indicative. Fuels: no unprocessed coal or kerosene indoors, as WHO advises. Every room with a combustion appliance gets a carbon monoxide alarm (EN 50291-1 or the local equivalent). Stoves that also heat (cold climates) are installed with clearances to combustible materials and a non-combustible hearth as the stove's instructions and local fire rules require. The build pack states fuel type, power output, test results and installation requirements.
