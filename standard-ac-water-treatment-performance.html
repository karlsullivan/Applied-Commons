Without a performance target there is no way to tell whether a water module protects health. The WHO scheme's star ratings are the international, health-based classification for household water treatment, and the drinking-water guidelines give the targets for the water itself. Failing safe matters because people trust a treated tap.

Specification

Water treatment modules are rated against the WHO International Scheme to Evaluate Household Water Treatment Technologies. 3-star: at least 4 log10 reduction of bacteria, 5 log10 of viruses and 4 log10 of protozoa. 2-star: 2, 3 and 2 log10. 1-star (targeted protection) only as part of a multi-barrier system, with the gap stated. Applied Commons treatment modules aim for 2-star at minimum and state which pathogen class is their weak point. Treated water meets the WHO Guidelines for drinking-water quality: E. coli not detectable in any 100 mL sample, and the chemical guideline values for contaminants known at the site, in particular arsenic 10 ug/L, fluoride 1.5 mg/L and nitrate 50 mg/L. Ratings come from testing to the WHO scheme's protocol, or from a laboratory using it; a field test (for example E. coli counts before and after with a portable kit) is labelled as indicative. The build pack states flow rate, treated volume before replacement or cleaning, the maintenance interval, and how the module fails (it should stop delivering water, not deliver untreated water). Where national rules are stricter (in the EU, Directive (EU) 2020/2184), they apply.
