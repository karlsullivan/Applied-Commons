Solar is the main energy source for off-grid modules, and the commonest PV failures (mismatched connectors, undersized cable, cold-weather overvoltage) are cheap to avoid. This revision corrects the charge-controller safety standard: IEC 62509 covers lead-acid controllers only, so IEC 62109-1 is the safety basis.

Specification

Solar panels connect to charge controllers or inverters as follows. Connectors per IEC 62852 (MC4-type), with plug and socket of the same type from the same manufacturer at every joint (IEC 60364-7-712): mixing makes, even ones sold as compatible, voids the certification and is a known cause of fires. Cable: PV cable to EN 50618 / IEC 62930 (H1Z2Z2-K), sized for the current and at most 3 % voltage drop. Voltage: the array's open-circuit voltage at the coldest expected cell temperature stays below the controller's or inverter's maximum input. Panel voltage rises in the cold, so cold-climate sites (Estonian winters reach about -30 C) calculate with the panel's temperature coefficient. Arrays of 100 W or more, or above 35 V open circuit, are designed to IEC 62548-1 and tested to IEC 62446-1 when installed; smaller module-level panels (sensor nodes) still use rated connectors and a fuse. A DC switch-disconnector of utilisation category DC-PV2 (IEC 60947-3) sits between the array and the controller; string fuses where three or more strings run in parallel. Charge controllers and inverters meet IEC 62109-1 for safety (inverters also IEC 62109-2); charge controllers for lead-acid batteries also meet IEC 62509 for performance. Frames and mounts are earthed where the local rules require, and mounts are designed for the site's wind and snow loads. The battery side follows ac-dc-bus-elv.
