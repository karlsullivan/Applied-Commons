The universal enclosure rating scheme. IP44 is cheap to reach with a gasketed box and proper cable entries: the difference between surviving a wet season and failing in two weeks. Testing a DIY enclosure in the build pack, rather than claiming a rating, keeps it honest without lab cost. Cold climates and sun add the failures IP codes do not cover: condensation inside sealed boxes, UV-embrittled plastic, cracking at -25 C.

Specification

Ingress protection per IEC 60529 (EN 60529, AS 60529): IP44 minimum for electronics exposed to splashing indoors or under cover; IP55 or better for outdoor, dusty or wet sites; IP67 for parts that can be submerged. Proof: a bought enclosure carries the manufacturer's marked rating and keeps it as installed (a rated gland or grommet on every cable entry, gasket intact, mounting holes outside the seal). A DIY or 3D-printed enclosure is given no IP rating; instead it passes the build pack's test step (a spray test for the splash tier, a hose test for the outdoor tier, 30 minutes at 1 m for submersible parts), with the result recorded. Outdoor durability: UV-stabilised material (polycarbonate, ASA or UV-stabilised polyester; not plain ABS or PLA outdoors); rated for the site's temperature range (-25 to +50 C for the cold-climate set); stainless or coated hardware; and a pressure-equalising breather vent or drain so condensation does not collect. Impact: IK08 per IEC 62262 for outdoor enclosures within reach of people or animals. North American deployments also state the NEMA 250 / UL 50E type (Type 3R minimum outdoors, Type 4X for hose-down or corrosive sites). Enclosures with mains inside also follow their own product standard; this standard covers ingress, durability and impact only.
