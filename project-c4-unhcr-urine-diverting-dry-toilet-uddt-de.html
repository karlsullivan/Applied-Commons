This project is under research: Applied Commons has catalogued it but has not written a build pack for it.

UNHCR public design and bill-of-quantities specification for a source-separating urine-diverting dry toilet with on-site treatment; detailed construction templates (urine diversion well, foot rests, chamber heights) and BoQ make it directly locally buildable for water-scarce and off-grid/refugee/emergency settings. Institutional, published, low-cost and service-chain-independent.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • D406-2025A 'stabilization period of up to 2 years... used safely as a fertilizer (WHO 2006)' is a design/BoQ statement, not climate-specific pathogen data. The 2-year figure comes from WHO (2006), where 1.5-2 years is the COLD-climate criterion (storage at 2-20 C): it 'removes' bacterial pathogens and 'reduces' viruses and protozoa, but WHO explicitly notes 'E-coli, Salmonella and soil-borne ova may persist' and that hand hygiene after excreta handling is still required even in warm conditions.
  • No peer-reviewed long-term FIELD study quantifying E. coli/Ascaris inactivation in passive UDDT vaults in wet, humid or cold climates was found. All field data are warm/dry (Dollo Ado/Hiloweyn Ethiopia; Vietnam; El Salvador; Panama). The most complete field study (Brown et al. 2024, CDC, Sci Total Environ, PMC13047511) concludes 'additional research in more temperate and humid environments is needed' - a direct statement that the warm/dry results do not generalize to the climates in question.
  • Measured in-vault E. coli outcome (CDC/UNHCR, Hiloweyn Camp, Ethiopia; seeded closed-vault longitudinal study): UDDTs whose vault samples contained <1000 E. coli/g total solids increased from 30% to 95% over 12 months of storage, and a >2.8-log10 reduction in Ascaris ova viability was observed after 6 months, under warm, dry, alkaline conditions.
  • Cold/temperate conditions slow Ascaris inactivation far more than bacteria. In a controlled 22 C / 45% RH lab (Magri et al. 2013, AEM, PMC3623211), Ascaris suum decimal-reduction time (Dr) was 120-192 days with ash, oyster-shell or urea cover, and 14-20% egg viability remained at day 120, whereas E. coli and phage inactivated within days. In a cold-winter design report (Richert Stintzing et al., WECF 2007), '35 C for 6 months is not reachable in cold winters; storage must be prolonged.'
  • Alkaline pH alone does not inactivate Ascaris when cold: Senecal, Nordin & Vinneras (2020, J Water Health 18(3):375-382) found that pH 10.5-12.5 at <=27.5 C for more than 70 days had no detectable effect on Ascaris viability, and that temperature - not pH - was the dominant driver.
  • The only quantified active treatment pathway using Nairobi UDDT waste - anaerobic co-fermentation with banana waste to produce undissociated carboxylic acids (Harroff et al. 2019, Front Environ Sci 7:139) - inactivated Ascaris within 15 days at pH 4.8-5.2, BUT the authors state a temperature >=30 C is required for the acid production to occur, so this pathway also does not function in cold winters. It is an off-site/active process, not a passive vault-desiccation substitute.
  • The UNHCR design (D406-2025A) and SOP (OG401-2018A) fix the siting as design rules: disposal/soakage pit >=30m from all water sources and >=60m from dwellings/camp facilities; the base of all latrine and infiltration pits >=1.5m above the maximum annual groundwater table; 'proper lining where the water table is close'. These are prescribed rules, NOT measured downstream outcomes.
  • pH amendment with hydrated lime accelerates Ascaris inactivation in WARM conditions but is not a cold-climate fix: Brown et al. 2024 showed 2-5% (w/w) hydrated lime holding pH>12 drove a >2-log Ascaris reduction at the first 7-day sampling (5% held pH 12.5-12.8 over 84 days), whereas at <=27.5 C the same alkalinity had no effect (Senecal 2020).
