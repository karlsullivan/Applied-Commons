Open-source (CC-BY-SA) CNC-fabricated structural-timber (plywood) building system: carbon-negative blocks, modular clip-together chassis assembled on site in days by teams without conventional construction skills, globally locally manufactured, designed for reuse/recycling. COLD-CLIMATE: its own docs include an 'extremely cold climate' insulation path (rigid + external woodfibre board to >0.15 W/m2K U-value) and an independent Loughborough University thermal-performance & life-cycle-assessment report across insulation/window/airtightness variables. Mature (community, manufacturer network, LCA study).

Testing

CheckHowPass if
Structural integrity under lateral (racking) load - a finished wall/module must sustain its design lateral load without progressive collapse (shelter + health: a safe structure).Cyclic racking test of the finished panels / a representative module, as run in the BRE programme (2D panels 5.4 m x 3.1 m; one module 5.3 m x 5.3 m x 3.1 m), loaded in 0.1F -> 0.4F -> to-failure cycles. For a deployed unit the control is a connection-by-connection inspection (every through-wall peg and bow-tie plate) before any load is applied.Sustains the design racking load with no progressive collapse. In the BRE programme the design racking target was 80 kN for a 5.4 m x 3.1 m wall panel and 50 kN per jack for the 5.3 m x 5.3 m module, and failure was defined as loss of 20% or more of the sustained load.
Thermal fabric - the completed wall/floor/roof must meet the design U-value / heat-transfer coefficient so winter heating demand is met and indoor temperatures stay safe (shelter + health: efficient insulation, safe low/high indoor temperatures).As-built U-value survey plus a blower-door airtightness test of the finished unit, or lab testing of the wall build-up across the insulation-product x window-type x airtightness matrix used in the Leeds Beckett sensitivity analysis.Wall insulation at the specified depth with no uninsulated gaps or cavities (Skylark 250 = 250 mm, Skylark 200 = 200 mm); the fabric reaches Passivhaus-class heat-transfer coefficient with triple glazing and the best airtightness level; for extremely cold climates add a continuous external rigid / woodfibre layer (the project's documented cold-climate path).
Fire resistance - wall/floor/roof assemblies must achieve the certified fire-resistance class (shelter + health: safe from fire spread).Standard fire-resistance test of the wall/floor/roof element per EN 1363-1, or use of the existing Efectis REI 60 / REI 90 certificates for the certified build-up (OSB panel + bio-based insulation + two 15 mm plasterboard layers on the fire-exposed face).Floors/roofs REI 60 and walls REI 90, each achieved with no failure - the 3 m-wide floor/roof specimen terminated at 61 min and the wall specimen at 91 min, both with no failure.
Moisture / airtightness / condensation - the wall build-up must stay dry so there is no mould, rot or loss of thermal performance, and indoor air quality is protected (shelter + health: indoor air quality, damp).Hygrothermal modelling plus on-site moisture measurement of the insulation and timber before the rainscreen is closed and again after a heating / wet season; a blower-door airtightness test to confirm the driving potential is controlled.No uninsulated gaps or cavities; continuous airtight and vapour-control layers; airtightness at the lowest level modelled in the thermal study (the level that reaches Passivhaus-class HTC); insulation and timber moisture within safe (dry) limits across a full heating and wet season.
Design for deconstruction / circularity - components must be recoverable and re-usable at end-of-life so a local salvage market forms (shelter + local economy: landfill-diversion target 65% or higher).A documented deconstruction trial of a finished module: record the disassembly sequence, the share of clip-together blocks recovered intact for re-use, and the fraction of C&D material diverted from landfill.The module disassembles with locally available tools, the blocks are recovered intact for re-use rather than demolished, and the measured C&D material diverted from landfill is 65% or higher.
Low embodied + life-cycle carbon - the finished unit must be measurably below the cement/steel baseline (climate requirement: spec in kg CO2e per m2).Life-cycle assessment (ISO 14040/44) of the finished unit, compared against a conventional brick-and-block cavity-wall house of equivalent fabric.Life-cycle CO2eq of the finished unit less than half that of the conventional brick-and-block cavity-wall house over a 100-year period; net carbon-negative, with CO2 stored in the timber (the LCA found roughly 100 kg CO2e/m2 total project vs 300-500 for brick, a net of about -350 kg CO2e/m2 stored).

Failure modes

FailureEffectMitigation
Through-wall peg and bow-tie plate disengagement (BRE racking tests).Loss of racking capacity and separation of wall elements - in the 2D window wall the bow-tie plates failed at a window corner and the through-wall peg to the foundation beam almost disengaged under uplift; in the 3D module the through-wall peg disconnected at failure. Progressive, potentially partial-collapse behaviour under lateral load.Inspect and verify every through-wall peg and bow-tie plate before and after assembly; use the designed fastening without substitution; apply loads only once the connection checklist is signed; re-check connections after any movement.
Localised compressive collapse at a point load (BRE solid wall).The solid wall failed locally under the concentrated racking load at the loading point, requiring repair at that point twice before a clean uplift + bow-tie failure was obtained.Distribute concentrated loads via bearing plates / noggins; avoid un-reinforced point loads on panels; keep openings to the designed apertures and verify capacity of any additional penetration.
Shear-tie snap / spreader-plate punching (BRE 3D module).A shear tie near the top right of the steel spreader plate snapped, with concern that the plate would punch through the wall; the second cycle had to be stopped at about 0.3F.Inspect shear ties and the spreader-plate region before loading a module; confirm tie capacity for the intended load; do not overload the loaded panel; replace / re-tie any damaged shear tie before continuing.
Bio-based insulation moisture ingress -> mould / rot (field: a two-year holiday build).Moisture trapped in the flax / hemp insulation leading to mould and potential rot, with loss of thermal performance; reported by a builder seeking advice.Provide a continuous airtight and vapour-control layer, a ventilated rainscreen, no uninsulated gaps, and a moisture check before the rainscreen is closed; in hot-humid climates use a robust rainscreen (see the hot-humid/coastal field report).
Overheating / durability in hot-humid and coastal climates (field: Rimini, Italy; bamboo plywood in the tropics).A lightweight timber system overheats faster than masonry if glazing is oversized or shading is skipped; salt air increases maintenance on exposed elements, so performance and durability are detailing-dependent rather than inherent.Treat external shading, glazing ratios and the night-purge ventilation strategy as core design inputs; use stainless / galvanised fixings and claddings with a known coastal track record; plan for facade maintenance.
Seismic behaviour unverified (field: Turkey-Syria earthquake request).An NGO requested WikiHouse for post-disaster (seismic) use, but there is no published seismic test, so the system's behaviour in an earthquake is unverified and unquantified.Do not deploy in high-seismic zones without a dedicated Eurocode 8 (EN 1998) check or a seismic test; treat seismic performance as a current gap until such a test or check is published.

Known gaps

What the original design does not document:

  • No WASH components: the Skylark BOM covers the building shell only (no water, sanitation, or electrical line items) - WASH requirements are entirely out of scope of the published BOM.
  • No e-waste / metals-recovery pathway: aluminium (reveals, gutters, flashings, doors) and steel (roofing, screws) are present in the BOM but no recovery or recycling route is specified beyond the timber stream.
  • No per-line unit prices or supplier quotes anywhere in the project's public record - unit_cost is null for every row by design; the cost basis is 'no published prices' (see cost_basis field).
  • Reference-year mismatch: the quantity table is from the July 2021 Innovate UK reference house (200/250 mm wall system) and predates the Skylark v1.0 open-source release (Dec 2024, 150/200 mm walls, different insulation stack); row-level quantities are order-of-magnitude for a current build, not an exact issue list.
  • Per-item embodied-carbon split is not published: the LCA reports GWP by life-cycle stage (A1-A3 ... C4), not by material line; the headline product-stage and 100-year totals appear only in the report's results bar chart, not its prose, so no per-material carbon figure is stated here.
  • Cladding fixings (row 16) carry no numeric quantity - the source BOM lists supplier 'Unknown' and 'Ignored due to negligible impact' (a carbon accounting note, not a statement that none are needed); size them to the ~294 m2 of cladding when costing.
  • External doors carry two figures in the source (2.64 m2 door + 0.87 m2 secondary); the JSON reports the primary 2.64 m2 - confirm against the LCA Table 3.3 at costing time.
  • No single-use-plastic reduction or recycling pathway is documented for the envelope (membranes are LDPE/HDPE/PP) - no plastics-circularity line exists in the BOM.
  • Skylark 200-series blocks (2x span) not yet released per the repo README.
  • Additional room spans / roof profiles / window-opening ranges beyond the 3-span, limited-roof, smaller-opening Skylark 150 set are not yet released.
  • Current Skylark parametric Grasshopper engine is not published in the repos (only the prior-generation Wren .gh is present).
  • No schematics / PCB / firmware exist upstream: WikiHouse is a structural timber chassis (no electronics), so these design categories are not applicable, not missing.
  • No published seismic test or Eurocode 8 check - seismic behaviour is unverified despite a documented post-disaster request.
  • No as-built U-value, airtightness, or indoor-air-quality / CO measurement on a completed, occupied unit - the thermal results are simulation (Leeds Beckett) and lab-based, not as-built.
  • No published cost-per-m2 comparison against prevailing local self-build cost in the brief's low-income / no-industrial-base contexts (the system needs a CNC router, grid power and imported ply/OSB).
  • No measured landfill-diversion figure at actual deconstruction - the 65%+ target is plausible / assumed, not measured.
  • No WASH components (pipes, storage tanks, latrines) - the system is a building chassis only, so the water + sanitation requirement is out of scope.
  • No e-waste / secondary-metals-recovery pathway - the electronics / digital requirement is out of scope.
  • No single-use-plastic reduction or local recycling pathway - the water + food + environment requirement is out of scope.
  • No measured long-term durability / service-life data in tropical, hot-humid or coastal conditions (qualitatively 'no inherent reason it cannot achieve a long service life', but not measured).
  • No extreme-wind / cyclone testing - the location & suitability guide excludes extreme-wind sites, but there is no published wind-load test.
  • No dedicated indoor-air-quality / smoke / CO test on a finished unit - the fire test addresses element fire resistance, not indoor air quality or CO protection.

Safety

Safe to build the shell only with a qualified structural engineer's sign-off, a verified fire-resistance (REI) check once the plasterboard lining is specified, and an Approved Document B fire-compliance assessment; the system is untested to Eurocode 8 (seismic) and the claimed REI depends on an internal lining that is not in the BOM. Wood-dust, silica, mineral-wool and CNC machine hazards are already controlled by the Manufacturing Guide.

HazardSeverityControls
Load-bearing walls/floors/roofs can fail or collapse under gravity, racking (wind/earthquake) or uplift. In the BRE racking programme the through-wall peg and bow-tie plate were the two dominant failure modes (peg nearly disengaged under uplift; plate failed at a window corner), giving progressive/partial-collapse behaviour. The Technical Specification states the system 'has not yet been tested for earthquake resistance' and that the engineer 'will probably have to conservatively assume nominal ductility'. Assembly by people without conventional construction skills raises the risk of an incorrectly engaged peg/plate.highEngage a qualified/chartered structural engineer to check the specific build and sign off the connections and any seismic assumption (EC8).; Do not deploy in high-seismic zones without a dedicated EN 1998 check or a seismic test (the spec's own documented gap).; Verify every through-wall peg is fully engaged and every bow-tie plate correctly fixed before any load is applied, and re-inspect all connections once the frame is complete (the two dominant BRE failure modes).; Follow the assembly sequence and torque/dowel specs in the build pack; do not substitute connectors.
Timber structure and insulation burn; fire resistance and reaction to fire must meet the building's requirements. The Technical Specification/product page claim walls 'certified' to REI 90 and floors/roofs to REI 60 (test specimens terminated at 91 and 61 min with no failure), but the spec cites no fire-test standard, report number or approver, and the achieved REI depends on a 'plasterboard or equivalent' internal lining that is NOT in the bill of materials - so the claim is not achievable as the BOM stands. Approved Document B additionally bans combustible materials in/on the external walls of buildings under the 2022 amendments, and lists timber frame as a 'large timber building / modern construction method' outside simple ADB guidance.highSpecify and install the plasterboard-or-equivalent internal lining (and any fire-stopping of penetrations) that the REI rating depends on; confirm the achieved REI from the actual test report before occupancy.; Obtain/confirm the fire-test standard, report number and approver behind the REI 90/60 claim (the spec does not state them).; Commission an Approved Document B fire-compliance assessment by a competent person, including the 2022 combustible-external-wall provisions, before occupation.
CNC cutting of OSB/plywood generates wood dust (carcinogenic; wood-dust OELs apply) and, for sawn/milled timber, respirable crystalline silica (silicosis). Mineral-wool insulation (Rockwool) is a nuisance/fiber inhalation hazard and is classified as a carcinogen under some schemes. OSB/ply can emit formaldehyde from adhesives. These are the dominant occupational health risks during fabrication and finishing.mediumUse the Manufacturing Guide's dust controls: cyclone/dust extraction at the source, local exhaust ventilation, and a formal risk assessment.; Wear the specified PPE (eye/ear protection, gloves, safe footwear, and a face mask/respirator for mineral wool and fine dust); use a P2/FFP2 or better respirator for silica-bearing work.; Confirm OSB/plywood is E1 (or equivalent) low-formaldehyde grade and ventilate during and after assembly; wet-clean or vacuum rather than dry-sweep.; Measure/assess silica exposure if any milled (non-pressed) timber or stone is cut.
CNC machinery presents cutting, entanglement, chip-ejection and noise hazards, and full OSB/ply sheets plus prefabricated wall/floor/roof blocks are heavy and awkward, creating manual-handling, pinch-point and lifting hazards on a site with no specialist lifting crew.mediumFollow the Manufacturing Guide's machine-safety provisions (interlocks, guards, pressure mats) and do not remove or bypass them; lock out before clearing jams.; Run a formal risk assessment for the specific machine and site.; Use the clip-together handling points and a lifting aid/two-person or mechanical lift for full sheets and blocks; plan the lift before picking up a block.
Building-physics moisture: trapped water/condensation in the insulated timber fabric causes insulation mould and timber rot (field-reported in the hot-humid/coastal deployment) - a durability and indoor-air-quality failure rather than a food/potable-water hazard (there is no potable water or food contact in a building shell).mediumInstall a continuous airtight and vapour-control layer with no uninsulated gaps or cavities.; Fit a ventilated rainscreen and carry out a damp/moisture check of the fabric BEFORE the rainscreen is closed.; In hot-humid/coastal climates use the robust rainscreen and the project's documented moisture path.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Minimum viable CNC for WikiHouse is any router whose bed fits a 1220x2440mm (4x8ft) sheet; the official manufacturing guide states 'in theory, WikiHouse parts can be manufactured anywhere you can fit a 4'x8' CNC router - even in a garage'.
  • Power draw (Maslow's official FAQ): 120-240V, 50-60Hz, '<1 Amp for the machine, 7 amps for the router' - a peak load of roughly 0.85 kW at 120V to ~1.7 kW at 240V; 70mm max cut depth in multiple passes; typical cut feed ~2,000 mm/min. The electronics draw is negligible next to the router.
  • No seismic/earthquake test data exists for WikiHouse Skylark. The official 2025 tech spec states the system 'has not yet been tested for earthquake resistance but it can still be used in seismic areas. Your structural engineer will probably have to conservatively assume zero ductility.' The only published large-scale structural test (BRE P120099-1000, 2021-22) was static racking only: 4 full-height panels (~5.4m x 3.1m, 80 kN target) plus one 3D module (5.3 x 5.3 x 3.1 m); failures were bow-tie plate failure and through-wall peg disengagement (uplift) — no cyclic/dynamic loading, no full-building seismic protocol, and no moisture/durability testing in the report.
  • Maslow-class low-tech kits are named in the official guide as the affordable prototyping tier ('For prototyping a low-tech hardware kit like a Maslow may be sufficient'); 'For production purposes (generally projects needing more than 50 sheets cut) a higher performance, gantry-based CNC router is necessary'. A single remote-house build fits the prototyping tier; multi-house operations need a gantry router.
  • The official durability claim is explicitly dryness-conditional: block materials 'typically have a certified life of 60 years, but provided blocks are kept dry and protected, it should last much longer than this. Ensure protection from weather, condensation, rodents and insect attack.' The spec mandates the chassis be lined internally with a vapour barrier 'to prevent condensation within the structure' and stresses high airtightness/MVHR. The 60-year figure is therefore a material certification, not a demonstrated in-situ service life — and its precondition (kept dry) is exactly what high-humidity/tropical sites stress; the one known in-situ moisture failure (finding above) occurred precisely where the vapour-control requirement was not met.
  • Thermal evidence is also prescriptive, with no in-situ tropical thermal-comfort data. Official guidance: Skylark 250 wall U-value 0.15 W/m2K (ultra-low energy); 'In hotter climates special attention must be paid to shading, ventilation and the introduction of additional thermal mass to prevent overheating'; the Principles of Good Design note the lightweight timber system has low thermal mass and can overheat in hot weather, mitigating with shading, orientation, glazing, and added mass. The system is 'well suited to temperate climates' and 'easily adapted to extreme cold' (add insulation), but 'not suited to sites with extreme wind loads' and 'not suitable for sites likely to flood ... where the timber chassis would be in the water'.
  • Newest quantitative structural data is lab-only and not seismic: a 2025 peer-reviewed study (Materials 18(20):4738, PMC12566597) on full-scale 18mm Pinus radiata plywood TIE joints in three variants (Standard, Weather-Resistant HR, Fire-Resistant FR) ran monotonic tensile and shear tests only. HR was strongest in tension (7.7 kN), FR strongest in shear (8.2 kN), and the fire-retardant treatment induced brittle fracture with significantly reduced ductility (strength-ductility trade-off) — relevant to seismic behaviour, where ductility is the governing demand, but no cyclic, fatigue or humidity-aging testing was performed.
  • Machine cost: the Maslow 4.1 kit is US$525.00 on the official shop (includes electronics, axes, belts, sled hardware; explicitly excludes the required DeWalt DWP611 / D26200 240V router and a flat rigid mounting surface; ships anywhere in the world). A 2022 third-party review (CNCSourced) quotes the earlier generation at ~$599. Budget ~$525-600 plus a $150-300 router for the cutting machine itself.
