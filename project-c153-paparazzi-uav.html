The safety review found that parts of this build need a qualified person.

Free, open-source (GPL) hardware and software platform for autonomous UAVs (flight controller, autopilot, ground station); low-cost, self-buildable and field-replaceable. Used by hobbyists, universities and companies on vehicles from 11.9 g to 25 kg. Cold-climate proof: the open-source fixed-wing Paparazzi UAV carried the survey camera for the Kanderfirn alpine glacier monitoring (high mountain, low air pressure, poor GPS), demonstrating open, low-cost aerial mapping hardware that operates in cold, harsh environments.

Known gaps

What the original design does not document:

  • No standardized acceptance-test procedure for a mapping mission exists in the upstream docs (the tutorial site's index is literally 'TBD'); accuracy targets come from ODM documentation and field papers, not from Paparazzi itself
  • No published end-to-end test of the need brief's full chain: Paparazzi -> ortho -> vector extraction / OSM tracing. Upstream stops at raster (orthophoto/DSM), so village/building basemap acceptance cannot be verified against an upstream-documented target
  • No upstream documentation of WASH point-data capture (annotation workflow, schema, local-language labels) on drone imagery; the WASH-side need is documented separately (Oxfam) but no drone-imagery-to-WASH-point pipeline ships with the project
  • No documented field deployment mode for the 'phone-first, no specialist GIS staff' requirement: the platform assumes a GCS computer and a trained operator; no offline phone-based ground-station build is documented
  • Output licensing of processed products (ODbL or otherwise) is not stated in any retrieved ODM/Paparazzi page; the ODbL compatibility assumption in the need brief is unverified
  • Cost documentation is fragmented: the Kanderfirn paper gives a full UAS cost (~1,300 EUR excluding GCS computer) and Mesquita et al. report $995 for their DIY build, but upstream has no bill-of-materials price list; component prices come only from the retailer list
  • ODM hardware guidance is scattered: the install doc recommends 16 GB RAM / 100 GB disk for a few hundred images (RAM table up to 256 GB at 5,000 images) while forum reports add RAM-as-bottleneck and throughput data; there is no single authoritative sizing table
  • Legal test criteria are US-centric in the retrieved sources (FAA Part 107); the target deployment's local UAS regulations (e.g., Africa/Asia jurisdictions) are not covered by the retrieved sources

Safety

Safe as designed for village/farm field trials once the listed controls are in place. The pack already covers the two highest-severity hazards (mechanical propellers/control surfaces and structural loss-of-control) with preflight inspection, VLOS, a 500 m cap and manual-RC backup. Remaining gaps are compliance- and discipline-driven, not design-defective: add jurisdiction-specific radio/UAS licensing, an explicit LiPo charging and thermal/fire storage procedure, and a fixed inspection cadence (after 10 flights or 2 weeks). No gas, pressure, food/water, or medical hazards are present (electric platform).

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • The 0.7-1.2 m XY / 0.7-2.1 m Z accuracy band is a measured ODM 0.4.1 RMSE from exactly the Paparazzi -> ODM pipeline (Kanderfirn case study, Groos et al. 2019): fixed-wing Paparazzi UAV with GoPro Hero 5, 135 +/- 20 m a.g.l. flight altitude, GSD 5.7-7.7 cm/pixel, georeferenced with dGNSS GCPs of <0.1 m accuracy. XY RMSE scaled with orthophoto size (0.7 m for smaller, 1.2 m for larger maps) and Z RMSE spanned 0.7-2.1 m. On identical imagery Pix4D achieved XY RMSE 0.3-0.5 m and Z RMSE 0.4-0.5 m; the authors attribute part of the ODM gap to its georeferencing being an affine transform (translation, scaling, reflection, rotation) that cannot correct radial 'doming'/'fishbowling' lens distortion.
  • RTK/dGNSS (u-blox ZED-F9P) is the most expensive and fully import-dependent line: bare module $127.42 (DigiKey), EVK-F9P-01 eval board $259.03, and a 3DR-sold ZED-F9P Advanced RTK kit with WiFi and compass $540.00; centimetre accuracy additionally requires a separate ground base station.
  • A documented low-cost fixed-wing UAV (2 m EPP foam airframe, STM32-class autopilot, GNSS, 915 MHz telemetry, 4 S LiPo, camera) has a total material cost of US$995 (November 2019 internet prices); the same paper's comparison table reports ~US$1,440 for comparable DIY builds and ~US$15,797 for commercial equivalents.
  • The Paparazzi native board Apogee (STM32F405RGT6 Cortex-M4 168 MHz, MPU-9150 9-DOF IMU, MPL3115A2 barometer, 3x UART, 6x PWM) is sold only by import vendors - Drotek (FR), 1BitSquared (US), Transition Robotics, Alibaba, TaoBao - per the official Paparazzi wiki; it is not locally fabbable without SMT reflow capability.
  • OpenDroneMap processing is the dominant and most variable component of time-to-first-usable-map, and it is hardware-bound (RAM first). Direct field reports: 1,436 images on 96 GB RAM at ULTRA/ULTRA quality took nearly 57 hours; 3,000+ images will not process on 16 GB RAM; ODM's Lightning cloud service caps a task at 3,000 images / 128 GB RAM. A non-specialist team should expect the ODM run to take many hours to ~2+ days depending on dataset size, image count, and RAM - not minutes.
  • GCP placement is a genuine skill and equipment gate for a survey-grade map: the practical minimum is 5 GCPs (four corners + center), 30-60 cm high-contrast targets, surveyed with an RTK/PPK GNSS rover for 1-3 cm accuracy. Without GCPs, consumer drone GPS gives 1-3 m horizontal and up to ~10 m vertical error, which disqualifies the output for engineering/survey/GIS-overlay work. An RTK/PPK-equipped drone can skip physical GCPs entirely, but that shifts the skill/equipment cost to the drone.
  • Realistic per-operator regulatory/training cost in the US (Part 107): $175 knowledge test + $5 drone registration (min ~$180), plus roughly $149 for a test-prep course and 13+ hours of video study and a 60-question test, for a first-time-operator total of about $330. Renewal is free (online every 24 months). This is a one-time per-person cost, separate from the (free) Paparazzi/ODM software skills, which are self-taught from open docs and tutorials.
  • The 0.7-1.2 m / 0.7-2.1 m RMSE is setup-specific, not an inherent ODM limit: a GCP-validated study of the same OpenDroneMap stack (WebODM/NodeODM) measured 3D CE90 of 3.2-35 cm across three GNSS quality tiers (consumer, mid-range, RTK + survey-grade GCP) on 122-176 image datasets, and ODM's own documentation states that with GCPs absolute accuracy improves to ~2.5x GSD horizontal and ~4x GSD vertical (~17 cm at 7 cm GSD). Without GCPs, ODM output degrades to the on-board GNSS: standard-GPS absolute horizontal accuracy 2-6 m and vertical 3-4x worse per ODM documentation, and raw GNSS 3D CE90 in the devonrama study spanned 1.1 m (RTK) to 191 m (consumer camera GPS).
