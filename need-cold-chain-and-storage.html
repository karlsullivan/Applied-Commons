Keeping what essential needs depend on cool, dry and safe in storage: off-grid refrigeration for food and medicines, evaporative and thermal cooling, airtight grain storage. Moving goods stays under Transport and logistics.

The need

Food: 470 million smallholder farmers (income down up to 15% from post-harvest loss); 5.6 billion people in developing countries (2009) face 40% fruit/vegetable losses; maize feeds 4.5+ billion people in 94 developing countries (Nepal RCT sample: 692 smallholder households, 89% report weevil, 69% moth). Health: children in 50+ Gavi-supported countries (end-2025: 82.5% of decentralised distribution points had full vaccine availability, ~1 in 6 inconsistent); 57 Gavi-eligible countries surveyed for cold chain equipment; 260,000 African children killed by malaria yearly whose future vaccine will need cold chain. Where: sub-Saharan Africa (rural electrification 28.7% in 2020; 53.3% overall electricity access; >38% of food losses at production/postharvest/processing) plus South/Southeast Asia (e.g., India loses ~$18.5B/yr to post-harvest waste).

Chronic and structural, not episodic. 526 Mt of food production (12% of global total) lost in 2017 from lack of effective refrigeration alone - enough to feed ~1 billion people (811 million are hungry); global FLW cost $936B/yr; developing-world perishable losses 23% vs 9% developed (IIR 2009); maize storage losses 15-30%+ (16.33% Rwanda, 19-23% Tanzania, 15-25.5% Ethiopia; up to 80% unfavorable conditions). Health: vaccines above 8C/20C or frozen are destroyed and can be unknowingly administered; 1.5M under-five deaths/yr avoidable via immunization dependent on cold chain. Field severity: most refrigerators in a Rwandan provincial survey were conformed but many uncalibrated; non-automated generators = fridge dead all morning after outage; only 43% household electricity in the province; 48.5% of Tanzanian facilities deviated from WHO temperature monitoring. Definition caveat: FAO's 37% SSA post-harvest loss estimate sits far above nationally representative self-reported maize losses of 1.4-5.9%, concentrated in under 1/5 of households - severity at the household level is severe but skewed.

What people do now

Perishables: sold at 'throw-away' prices before spoiling (tomatoes ~2 days ambient); evening small-farm milk in most SSA countries cannot be sold reliably and goes to unreliable hawkers or laborious processing; farmers with grid-tied solar cold rooms pay ~$0.23/crate/day (ColdHubs: $27,000 capex, $835/yr opex, 6-yr battery life). Grain: maize stored in open sacks/racks/bamboo structures exposed to rain and pests (only 2% use improved storage in the Nepal study; avg loss 44 kg/household/season); improved option costs ~$2.50 per 50 kg PICS bag. Where grid exists: conventional vaccine refrigerators with manual chart monitoring and backup generators that often don't auto-start; monitoring devices often uncalibrated. Cost of the status quo: up to 15% of smallholder farm income; 1 in 6 vaccine points with inconsistent supply.

Where existing solutions fall short

Cost gap for off-grid mechanical cooling: the practical field floor is ~$40,000 for a solar walk-in cold room vs the ~$10,000 viability threshold (turnkey containers ~$100,000); the 2025 IIR/Efficiency-for-Access local-materials blueprint (-20% cost, -63% embedded carbon) is a design path, not yet a mass-distributed product. Health: up to 90% of facilities in 57 countries lacked reliable equipment; existing equipment is often uncalibrated with backup power that fails when unattended - real-time monitoring (43 countries) cut vaccine losses up to 80%, showing the sensor/maintenance layer is the cheap, high-value gap. Maintenance/skills: poor local engineering capacity drives downtime; donors historically fund stand-alone cold stores without integrated-cold-chain management. Grain: a proven $2.50 bag reaches only ~2% of farmers, held back by upfront cost, shelling labour and input-market distance. Data: top-down vs self-reported loss estimates differ by an order of magnitude, so intervention economics are routinely mis-specified. Evaporative/thermal cooling: no standardised, locally buildable product line bridging the zero-energy tier to mechanical systems, and no integrated design that lets one site use both.

What a solution must do

  • Health (vaccine/medicine potency): hold 2-8 C continuously through multi-day grid outages (backup power or thermal inertia sized to local outage frequency), with continuous temperature monitoring that records and alarms excursions - the monitoring/calibration gap, not just the fridge, is the documented field failure mode
  • Health (energy): off-grid duty at <=10% of conventional vaccine-fridge power draw (super-efficient unit class) so a small PV array + battery suffices for a rural health post
  • Food (perishable shelf life): pre-cool and hold at/under crop-specific optimum, exploiting Q10 (10 C colder ~2x shelf life): tomatoes ~2 days -> ~3 weeks; fish a few hours at 30 C -> ~10 days at 0 C; respect chilling-injury floors (~10 C for many tropical crops)
  • Food (market-scale capacity/cost): serve a 200-1,000 kg/day smallholder aggregation point with ~2 t storage at total installed cost well under the observed ~$40,000 floor, targeting the ~$10,000 level where economics pencil out, with user fees <=~$0.23/crate/day (at that fee trader profit rose 69%)
  • Food (grain, airtight/dry): hermetically store >=50 kg of shelled grain for 6-12 months without fumigation, cutting the 20-30% loss baseline by ~1/3 (~10 kg/bag, causal estimate) at ~$2.50/bag (a 150 kg holding ~$7.50)
  • Food (zero-electricity tier): evaporative/thermal option - double-wall wet-sand (ZECC) or daytime-solar ice/thermal storage - built from local materials, no grid, for short-shelf-life produce in hot dry climates
  • All (maintainability): maintainable by local technicians with regional parts supply; include calibration of temperature monitoring devices, since uncalibrated ageing equipment is the dominant field failure and farmers cannot maintain imported systems
  • All (climate/sustainability): low-GWP refrigerants; where built locally, natural insulation - the reference low-carbon cold room cut embedded emissions 63% and cost 20% vs polyurethane-panel design; standalone solar saves ~4x GHG over 20 years vs grid even in Kenya

Constraints

  • Off-grid power base: rural electrification 28.7% (SSA, 2020); only 53.3% overall electricity access - battery replacement every ~6 years is standing opex
  • Viability is utilisation-driven: at 70% utilisation (vs 94% observed) cold-room IRR falls 36%->25% and loan tenor extends to 5+ years; demand must be surveyed and units sized to it
  • Cooling alone does not work: cooling-as-a-service failed as a standalone model in field pilots; the unit must be integrally tied to market access/offtaker (full value-chain business model)
  • Crop seasonality: 1-2 month harvest windows leave single-purpose cold rooms idle most of the year; surplus power needs secondary income use
  • Evaporative cooling is wet-bulb-limited: only effective in dry climates and suits non-climacteric, high-humidity-tolerant produce; not a humid-equatorial solution
  • Chilling injury: many tropical crops are damaged below ~10 C - setpoints are crop-specific, 'colder is better' is false
  • Hermetic bags require shelled grain and closing labour; adoption collapsed where labour was scarce; 50% subsidy produced no significant loss reduction vs 100% subsidy
  • High per-site transaction costs: each deployment needs local socioeconomic/agricultural context work
  • Loss estimates are contested: use both the top-down FAO range (37% of SSA food; 8-12% handling/storage) and the self-reported 1.4-5.9% maize range when costing interventions
