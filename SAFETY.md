# Safety

Treat published designs, software, and results as experimental unless their suitability for a specific use has been demonstrated. A successful test is evidence only for the conditions tested.

## Before and during experiments

- Identify hazards, required expertise, controls, and stop conditions before starting.
- Consider chemicals, heat, pressure, electricity, moving parts, emissions, waste, and the test environment as applicable.
- Use suitable facilities and competent supervision. Do not proceed when hazards or controls are unclear.
- Record incidents, unexpected behaviour, failures, and changes to the method. Stop when conditions exceed the planned limits.
- Report validation limits alongside results so others can judge whether replication is appropriate.

## MineDetector and safety-critical work

MineDetector is incubation-only. Keep experiments to controlled settings using inert, non-hazardous surrogates. Do not handle suspected mines or unexploded ordnance, or use experimental systems in suspected hazardous areas.

Experimental detections, maps, and negative results must not be used to declare land safe, guide clearance, or support operational safety decisions. Real-world use requires appropriate specialist validation and engagement with qualified humanitarian mine-action organisations. Bench results alone do not establish field readiness.

## Reporting a concern

Raise a repository issue describing the affected material, observed problem, and known limitations, without publishing sensitive locations or hazardous operational detail. If public reporting would create risk, contact a maintainer privately through an available contact channel. Pause affected work until the concern is understood and controls are reviewed.
