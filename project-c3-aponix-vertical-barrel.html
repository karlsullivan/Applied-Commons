This project is parked: Applied Commons has catalogued it but has not written a build pack for it.

The Aponix Vertical Barrel is a modular, 3D-printable vertical growing system that runs in hydroponic, aquaponic or terraponic (substrate shelf) mode. The main elements of its V2 design were released under CERN-OHL-S-2.0. It grows leafy greens and herbs on a small floor area, indoors or outdoors.

Evidence

The strongest findings from Applied Commons research on this project:

  • Cost: the full standing soil-based (substrate) 6-segment barrel bundle is priced at 240.00 EUR (approx. 60 cm dia x 1.15 m).
  • No Aponix-published measured yield (kg per cycle or kg per year) for a standalone 7-10 level barrel exists in public sources. Aponix publishes only plant-density planning figures: 12 grow spaces per 15 cm level, 57 cm barrel diameter (~1 m2 footprint), 144 plants per m2 of floor at 2.30 m height, and a stated planning rule of ~525-630 m2 of barrel space needed for a daily yield of 1,000 heads of lettuce. Any per-barrel yield figure must be extrapolated from the system geometry and comparable-system measurements.
  • Geometry and part cost of a standalone barrel (retrieved from Aponix shop and key-features pages): 6 ring-segment parts per level, 12 grow spaces (2 netpot pieces) per level; 7 levels = 42 parts, 84 ports, 1.05 m; 10 levels = 60 parts, 120 ports, 1.50 m. Parts cost EUR 4.50-5.20 each injection-molded (3D print data free): 7-level parts EUR 189-218, 10-level parts EUR 270-312. Irrigation is gravity-fed at 1-2 L/min per barrel with ~10 min cleaning per cycle (no per-barrel pump required).
  • The substrate (V2) barrel's primary mode is a passive wicking bed and operates without electricity: a 50-60 L base of pH-neutral lava rocks/hydroton irrigates the soil upward by capillary action, an overflow valve keeps standing water from touching the soil, and a gravity fill port (tanking L) refills it - no pump, timer, or power needed.
  • A pump-free gravity top-watering option is also documented (a top water buffer between the top ring and lid; 'low tech irrigation (open hose) ... or just pouring a bucket on top'), so electricity-free watering is available in more than just wick mode.
  • No published replacement rates exist per barrel per year for water use, substrate, or plant matter. The manual only says 'from time to time add new compost or fertilizer'; generic wicking-bed sources treat organic-matrix decomposition as a feature managed by periodic compost top-ups and nitrogen supplementation, but no per-year-per-barrel figures are published.
  • Reference design: 57 cm diameter cylinder, 15 cm ring height, 6 segments per ring, 0-12 grow spaces per ring using 2in/5cm netpots, plant spacing 15 cm horizontal x 30 cm vertical at max density; a 12-ring stack = 72 parts + dome lid + base = 144 grow spaces, ~2.0 m tall, ~3 sqm; food-grade PVC-U + ASA, <100 L/h per barrel, timer irrigation 5-15 min spray / 45-55 min pause, <=1 cycle/h by day.
  • Best measured per-port lettuce yields on comparable vertical tower systems: 52.2 g (student 2-pipe tower) and 66.9 g (commercial tower) fresh leaf weight per 20 cm-diameter compartment, harvested at ~50 days, in a peer-reviewed controlled-environment study (JAES 11(2), 2022, Eastern KY); and 100-175 g per plant per ~30-day cycle (Agrotonomy vendor yield guide) up to 180-260 g head weights / 45-65 g per port per week over a 28-day cycle (Green & Greener, 400-plant sample). The ~25% shortfall of the student tower under identical conditions shows light distribution is a first-order yield variable. Working bracket: 67 g (floor) to 200 g (high) per port per cycle.
